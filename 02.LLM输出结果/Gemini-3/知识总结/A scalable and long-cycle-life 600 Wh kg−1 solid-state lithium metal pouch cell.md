## Research Background and Motivation

This research aims to address the critical challenges facing solid-state lithium metal batteries (SSLMBs): (1) Low ionic conductivity of solid polymer electrolytes (SPEs) at room temperature; (2) Poor interfacial contact between solid electrolyte and electrodes due to rigid solid-solid interfaces; (3) Lithium dendrite growth causing short circuits. Existing solid polymer electrolytes suffer from insufficient ionic conductivity (<10⁻⁵ S cm⁻¹) at room temperature and inability to maintain stable interfaces during cycling. This research is necessary to develop practical solid-state batteries with high energy density (>500 Wh kg⁻¹) and long cycle life for electric vehicle and grid storage applications.

### Polymerization Method

- **Polymerization Type**: Solution casting method (not in-situ polymerization)
- **Polymerization Conditions**: The PEO-LiTFSI matrix is prepared by solution casting, then plasticized with FEE solvent and LiNO₃ additive to form a plastic crystal solid electrolyte (PCSE)

### Key Innovation Points

1. **Plastic Crystal Solid Electrolyte (PCSE)**: Developed a PEO-based plastic crystal electrolyte plasticized with fluorinated ether (FEE), achieving high ionic conductivity (6.3 × 10⁻⁴ S cm⁻¹ at 25°C) through plastic crystal phase transition
2. **Gradient Interface Design**: Established a gradient interface comprising crystalline plastic crystal region, transition region, and amorphous region that accommodates volume changes and maintains continuous contact
3. **High-Voltage Cathode Compatibility**: Enabled stable operation with LiNi₀.₈Co₀.₁Mn₀.₁O₂ (NCM811) cathode through LiNO₃-induced stable CEI formation, achieving 4.5 V operating voltage

### Special Performance

- **Wide-Temperature Performance**: The PCSE maintains high ionic conductivity from -20°C to 60°C, enabling operation across wide temperature range. At -20°C, conductivity is 1.1 × 10⁻⁴ S cm⁻¹
- **Safety**: Non-flammable characteristics demonstrated by direct ignition test; excellent thermal stability with no decomposition below 200°C
- **High Specific Energy**: The 1 Ah pouch cell achieves gravimetric energy density of 621 Wh kg⁻¹ (based on cathode active material) and ~500 Wh kg⁻¹ (based on total cell mass)
- **Practical Scalability**: Successfully scaled from lab-scale coin cells to 1 Ah pouch cells with consistent performance

## Mechanism Analysis and Characterization

The research employed comprehensive characterization to elucidate the operating mechanisms:

**Phase Transition Behavior**: Differential scanning calorimetry (DSC) revealed that FEE plasticization reduces PEO crystallinity and induces plastic crystal phase transition at 15-25°C, which is crucial for high ionic conductivity. Variable-temperature XRD confirmed reversible phase transitions between crystalline, plastic crystal, and amorphous states.

**Gradient Interface Formation**: Cryo-SEM cross-sectional imaging showed a three-layer gradient structure at the Li|electrolyte interface: (1) Crystalline plastic crystal region (~5 μm) providing mechanical strength; (2) Transition region with mixed crystalline/amorphous phases; (3) Amorphous region (~2 μm) ensuring intimate contact. This gradient accommodates volume changes during cycling.

**Stable CEI Formation**: X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS) depth profiling demonstrated that LiNO₃ additive decomposes to form Li₃N and LiNₓOᵧ species in the cathode electrolyte interphase (CEI), which suppresses transition metal dissolution and enables stable high-voltage operation up to 4.5 V.

**Lithium Deposition Morphology**: In-situ optical microscopy and post-mortem SEM showed dense, dendrite-free lithium deposition morphology with uniform current distribution due to the gradient interface structure.

**Molecular Dynamics Simulation**: MD simulations revealed that FEE molecules coordinate with Li⁺ ions, reducing the energy barrier for Li⁺ transport and facilitating ion transport through the plastic crystal phase transition.

## Conclusions and Significance

This research demonstrates a practical pathway for high-energy-density solid-state lithium metal batteries through a plastic crystal solid electrolyte design. The key achievements include:

1. **High Ionic Conductivity**: PCSE achieves 6.3 × 10⁻⁴ S cm⁻¹ at 25°C, comparable to liquid electrolytes, through plastic crystal phase transition mechanism
2. **Stable Interface**: Gradient interface design solves the solid-solid contact problem, enabling 2000-hour stable cycling in Li||Li symmetric cells
3. **High-Energy Pouch Cells**: 1 Ah NCM811||Li pouch cells achieve 621 Wh kg⁻¹ energy density with 88.6% capacity retention after 200 cycles
4. **Scalability**: Successful demonstration from coin cells to practical pouch cells validates commercial feasibility

The significance lies in providing a scalable solid-state battery platform that combines high energy density, long cycle life, and improved safety without requiring complex interface engineering or external pressure. The plastic crystal electrolyte design strategy opens new avenues for developing practical solid-state batteries for electric vehicles and grid energy storage applications.
