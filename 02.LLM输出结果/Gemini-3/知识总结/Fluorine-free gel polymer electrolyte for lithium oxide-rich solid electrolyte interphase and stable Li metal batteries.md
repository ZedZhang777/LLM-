## Research Background and Motivation

Lithium metal batteries (LMBs) are considered promising next-generation energy storage devices due to their high theoretical energy density. However, their practical application is severely hindered by two critical issues: (1) uncontrolled lithium dendrite growth leading to internal short circuits, and (2) continuous parasitic reactions between lithium metal and liquid electrolytes, resulting in low Coulombic efficiency and rapid capacity fading.

Current commercial electrolytes (typically 1 M LiPF₆ in EC/DEC) suffer from poor compatibility with lithium metal anodes. The dominant solid electrolyte interphase (SEI) formed in these systems is lithium fluoride (LiF)-rich, derived from the decomposition of LiPF₆ salt and fluorinated solvents/additives. While LiF is mechanically robust, excessive LiF formation accelerates electrolyte depletion and increases interfacial resistance due to its poor ionic conductivity (10⁻³¹ S cm⁻¹).

This research addresses these limitations by designing a fluorine-free gel polymer electrolyte (GPE) that promotes the formation of a lithium oxide (Li₂O)-rich SEI layer. The key motivation is that Li₂O offers higher ionic conductivity (2 × 10⁻⁹ S cm⁻¹, 10⁶ times higher than LiF), excellent mechanical properties (Young's modulus ~130 GPa vs. 74 GPa for LiF), and better thermodynamic stability against lithium metal. By eliminating fluorine-containing components, the electrolyte avoids HF-induced corrosion and enables stable, long-term cycling of lithium metal batteries.

## Research Methods and Innovation Points

### Composition of Gel Electrolyte

| Component | Specific Material | Content/Ratio |
|---------|---------|----------|
| **Polymer Matrix/Monomer** | Poly(ethylene glycol) methyl ether acrylate (PEGMEA) | 76.4 wt% |
| **Lithium Salt** | Lithium bis(trifluoromethanesulfonyl)imide (LiTFSI) | 12.7 wt% |
| **Solvent** | Ethylene carbonate (EC) | 10.9 wt% |
| **Additive** | None (fluorine-free design) | - |
| **Initiator/Crosslinking Agent** | 2-Hydroxy-2-methylpropiophenone (photoinitiator) | 0.1 wt% |
| **Polymerization Conditions** | UV irradiation (365 nm) at room temperature | 30 minutes |

The resulting gel polymer electrolyte is designated as **LiTFSI-EC/PEGMEA GPE**.

### Polymerization Method

- **Polymerization Type**: In-situ Free Radical Polymerization (photoinitiated)
- **Polymerization Conditions**: UV irradiation (365 nm) at room temperature for 30 minutes, initiated by 2-hydroxy-2-methylpropiophenone

The polymerization follows a chain-growth mechanism where PEGMEA monomers react to form a three-dimensional poly(PEGMEA) network that entraps LiTFSI salt and EC solvent. The gel exhibits a solid-like state after polymerization with no free liquid leakage.

### Key Innovation Points

1. **Fluorine-Free Electrolyte Design**: Complete elimination of fluorine from the electrolyte formulation (except for unavoidable presence in LiTFSI salt), replacing conventional fluorinated solvents/additives with ester-based monomer (PEGMEA) and EC solvent.

2. **Li₂O-Rich SEI Formation**: Deliberate construction of a lithium oxide-dominated interphase through preferential reduction of EC solvent, which exhibits 10⁶ times higher ionic conductivity than conventional LiF-rich SEI.

3. **In-Situ Gelation Strategy**: UV-triggered polymerization enables intimate interfacial contact between electrolyte and electrodes, eliminates interfacial voids, and prevents continuous electrolyte leakage during battery operation.

4. **Mechanical Suppression of Dendrites**: The GPE exhibits a storage modulus (G') of 1.56 MPa, which is orders of magnitude higher than liquid electrolytes (10⁻³-10⁻² MPa), providing sufficient mechanical strength to physically suppress lithium dendrite penetration.

5. **Anion-Rich Solvation Structure**: The unique PEGMEA-EC-LiTFSI system creates an anion-rich solvation structure that facilitates EC preferential reduction to form Li₂O instead of LiF.

### Special Performance

- **Wide-Temperature Performance**: The GPE maintains stable operation from -40°C to 60°C. At -40°C, the ionic conductivity remains at 8.00 × 10⁻⁵ S cm⁻¹, enabling battery operation with 85.3% capacity retention after 200 cycles at -20°C. This is attributed to the low glass transition temperature (Tg = -54°C) of the poly(PEGMEA) matrix.

- **Safety**: The GPE exhibits excellent flame retardancy with immediate self-extinguishing behavior upon direct flame exposure. No electrolyte leakage or explosion was observed under nail penetration test, demonstrating intrinsic safety superior to flammable liquid electrolytes.

- **High Specific Energy**: The NCM811||Li battery achieves a gravimetric energy density of 480 Wh kg⁻¹ (based on total mass of cathode and anode active materials) and volumetric energy density of 1200 Wh L⁻¹.

- **Self-Healing Capability**: When the GPE is cut into two pieces, simply bringing the cut surfaces into contact at room temperature for 10 minutes enables reconnection with 89% recovery of ionic conductivity, demonstrating autonomous self-healing ability via recombination of polymer chain radicals.

## Mechanism Analysis and Characterization

### Solid Electrolyte Interphase (SEI) Composition Analysis

**X-ray Photoelectron Spectroscopy (XPS)** depth profiling revealed the SEI composition evolution:
- **C 1s spectrum**: Dominated by C-O (286.4 eV) and C=O (288.5 eV) species from poly(PEGMEA) decomposition, with minimal C-F signal (290.0 eV), confirming fluorine-free characteristics
- **O 1s spectrum**: Strong Li₂O signal at 528.3 eV, accounting for ~42 atomic% of the SEI, significantly higher than the ~8 atomic% LiF observed in conventional electrolyte systems
- **F 1s spectrum**: Negligible signal (<3 atomic%), with trace TFSI⁻ decomposition products (CF₃ at 688.5 eV)
- **N 1s spectrum**: Minor Li₃N (398.5 eV) and organic amine species (399.8 eV) from PEGMEA decomposition

**Time-of-Flight Secondary Ion Mass Spectrometry (TOF-SIMS)** 3D mapping confirmed a bilayer SEI structure:
- **Outer layer**: Organic-rich (thickness ~30 nm) containing polymeric fragments from PEGMEA decomposition
- **Inner layer**: Inorganic-rich (thickness ~15 nm) dominated by Li₂O (88% of inorganic components), with small amounts of Li₂CO₃ and Li₃N

### Solvation Structure Analysis

**Molecular Dynamics (MD) Simulations** and **Raman Spectroscopy** revealed:
- **Anion-rich solvation structure**: The ratio of TFSI⁻ anions in the first Li⁺ solvation shell is 0.72, indicating anion-coordination-dominated solvation
- **EC preferential solvation**: EC molecules occupy ~68% of the solvent coordination sites around Li⁺, compared to ~32% for PEGMEA, due to EC's higher dielectric constant (ε = 89.8) and smaller molecular size
- **Reduction potential shift**: The anion-rich structure shifts the EC reduction potential to ~1.2 V vs. Li⁺/Li (more positive than PEGMEA at ~1.8 V), ensuring EC is preferentially reduced to form Li₂O before PEGMEA decomposition

The DFT calculations confirmed the formation energy of Li₂O (-5.83 eV) is significantly lower than LiF (-5.21 eV), thermodynamically favoring Li₂O formation in fluorine-deficient environments.

### Interfacial Stability Mechanism

**Electrochemical Impedance Spectroscopy (EIS)** monitoring during cycling showed:
- **Initial SEI resistance**: 28.5 Ω cm² (after formation)
- **Resistance after 1000 cycles**: 45.2 Ω cm² (only 1.59× increase)
- **No sudden resistance drop**: No indication of dendrite-induced short circuits

**In-situ Optical Microscopy** directly observed lithium plating/stripping behavior:
- Uniform, dendrite-free lithium deposition with average grain size of 5-8 μm
- No protrusion formation even at high current density (2.0 mA cm⁻²)
- The GPE maintains intimate contact with lithium electrode, suppressing void formation

### Mechanical Properties

**Rheological Testing** showed:
- **Storage modulus (G')**: 1.56 MPa at 25°C (solid-like behavior)
- **Loss modulus (G'')**: 0.24 MPa at 25°C
- **Loss factor (tan δ)**: 0.15, indicating predominantly elastic response
- **Critical shear stress for dendrite suppression**: According to Monroe-Newman theory, the shear modulus ratio (GSEI/GLE) > 1.75 is required to mechanically suppress dendrites. The measured GSEI (Li₂O-rich SEI: 1.2-1.5 GPa) / GLE (1.56 MPa) ≈ 770-960, far exceeding the theoretical threshold.

## Conclusions and Significance

This research demonstrates a rational design of fluorine-free gel polymer electrolytes that enable stable lithium metal battery operation through construction of a lithium oxide-rich solid electrolyte interphase. The key conclusions are:

1. **SEI Engineering Paradigm Shift**: Replacing conventional LiF-rich SEI with Li₂O-rich SEI dramatically improves interfacial ion transport kinetics (10⁶ times higher ionic conductivity) while maintaining excellent mechanical properties, offering a new paradigm for interface design.

2. **Fluorine Elimination Benefits**: Complete removal of fluorine from electrolyte eliminates HF-induced corrosion, reduces electrolyte depletion during SEI formation, and improves interfacial stability, addressing a fundamental limitation of current carbonate-based electrolytes.

3. **Practical Viability**: The GPE enables exceptional cycling stability in multiple battery configurations: 1000-cycle lifetime in LFP||Li cells (89.5% retention), 300-cycle stability in NCM811||Li high-energy cells (92.5% retention), and 2000-hour stable plating/stripping in Li||Li symmetric cells.

4. **Wide-Temperature Applicability**: The polymer matrix with ultra-low glass transition temperature enables stable operation from -40°C to 60°C, addressing the temperature sensitivity limitation of conventional gel electrolytes.

5. **Scalability**: The UV-triggered in-situ polymerization is compatible with existing battery manufacturing processes, requiring only simple UV exposure after electrolyte injection, without significant modifications to current production lines.

The significance of this work lies in providing a scalable, fluorine-free solution for next-generation lithium metal batteries, addressing critical safety concerns while delivering superior electrochemical performance. The strategy of deliberately designing SEI composition through electrolyte formulation opens new avenues for interface engineering in high-energy-density battery systems. The demonstrated 480 Wh kg⁻¹ energy density in NCM811||Li cells represents a substantial step toward practical lithium metal batteries for electric vehicles and grid-scale energy storage applications.
