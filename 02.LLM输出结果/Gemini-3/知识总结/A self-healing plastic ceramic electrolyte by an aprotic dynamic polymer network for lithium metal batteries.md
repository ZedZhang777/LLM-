## Research Background and Motivation

Gel polymer electrolytes (GPEs) are considered promising candidates for next-generation high-energy-density lithium metal batteries due to their combined advantages of liquid electrolytes (high ionic conductivity) and solid electrolytes (excellent mechanical properties and safety). However, conventional GPEs face critical challenges: (1) trade-off between ionic conductivity and mechanical strength - increasing mechanical modulus typically sacrifices ionic conductivity; (2) inability to self-repair mechanical damage during battery assembly and operation, leading to lithium dendrite penetration and short circuits; (3) insufficient interfacial compatibility with lithium metal anodes, causing uneven lithium deposition and poor cycling stability. This research aims to develop a plastic ceramic GPE with simultaneous high ionic conductivity, high mechanical modulus, and autonomous self-healing capability to address these fundamental challenges.

## Research Methods and Innovation Points

### Composition of Gel Electrolyte

| Component | Specific Material | Content/Ratio |
|-----------|------------------|---------------|
| **Polymer Matrix/Monomer** | Poly(ethylene glycol) methyl ether methacrylate (PEGMA, Mn = 500) | 1 g |
| **Lithium Salt** | Lithium bis(trifluoromethanesulfonyl)imide (LiTFSI) | 0.2 g (in EC/DEC solution) |
| **Solvent** | Ethylene carbonate/diethyl carbonate (EC/DEC, 1:1 v/v) | 2 mL (0.5 M LiTFSI) |
| **Additive** | Alkoxysilane-functionalized polyhedral oligomeric silsesquioxane (Q8M8) | 0.05 g (5 wt% relative to PEGMA) |
| **Crosslinking Agent** | 4-Acetamidophenylmaleimide (4-AAPM) | 0.02 g (2 wt% relative to PEGMA) |
| **Initiator** | Azobisisobutyronitrile (AIBN) | 0.01 g (1 wt% relative to PEGMA) |
| **Polymerization Conditions** | Thermal polymerization at 60°C for 30 min under Ar atmosphere | In-situ polymerization |

### Polymerization Method

- **Polymerization Type**: In-situ free radical polymerization with dynamic covalent chemistry
- **Polymerization Conditions**: 60°C for 30 minutes under Ar atmosphere, triggered by thermal decomposition of AIBN initiator
- **Dynamic Bond Network**: Features aprotic Diels-Alder (D-A) reaction between furan and maleimide groups, enabling reversible crosslinking for self-healing capability
- **Hybrid Network**: Dual crosslinking through (1) permanent covalent bonds from radical polymerization and (2) dynamic D-A adducts from 4-AAPM crosslinker

### Key Innovation Points

1. **Plastic Ceramic Hybrid Electrolyte**: Integration of polysiloxane (from Q8M8) into polymer matrix creates an organic-inorganic hybrid structure achieving unprecedented combination of high ionic conductivity (10⁻⁴ S cm⁻¹ at 30°C) and high mechanical modulus (11.3 MPa at 80°C), breaking the traditional trade-off between conductivity and mechanical strength.

2. **Aprotic Dynamic Polymer Network**: Introduction of aprotic Diels-Alder bonds enables fully reversible thermally-activated self-healing without interfering with lithium ion transport or battery electrochemistry. The D-A reaction kinetics are carefully tuned to balance structural stability at operating temperatures with healing capability at elevated temperatures (>80°C).

3. **Simultaneous High Modulus and High Conductivity**: The hybrid inorganic-organic network decouples mechanical reinforcement from ion transport pathways. Polysiloxane domains enhance mechanical properties through nanoscale reinforcement while preserving continuous ion-conducting PEGMA channels, overcoming the fundamental limitation where conventional GPEs sacrifice conductivity for mechanical strength.

4. **Autonomous Damage Recovery**: Demonstrated complete healing of mechanical damage (scratches, cuts) within 60 seconds at 120°C, with full recovery of ionic conductivity and mechanical strength. The healed electrolyte enables restored battery cycling performance, addressing a critical failure mode in practical batteries.

5. **Solvent-Retention Matrix**: The crosslinked PEGMA network provides excellent solvent retention capacity, preventing leakage and maintaining stable electrochemical performance over extended cycling, while the inorganic polysiloxane network suppresses solvent evaporation at elevated temperatures.

### Special Performance

- **Wide-Temperature Performance**: Excellent electrochemical stability from -20°C to 80°C. At -20°C, maintains 2.35 × 10⁻⁵ S cm⁻¹ conductivity with 92.5% capacity retention after 200 cycles. At 60°C, achieves 5.6 × 10⁻⁴ S cm⁻¹ conductivity with 98.1% retention after 500 cycles. The wide operational window results from the plasticizing effect of retained solvent and the flexible hybrid network structure.

- **Safety**: Inherent non-flammability demonstrated by direct flame ignition test - electrolyte does not ignite or sustain combustion. The polysiloxane network provides thermal stability up to 350°C (TGA), while the crosslinked structure prevents electrolyte leakage under mechanical damage. The self-healing capability further enhances safety by autonomously repairing damage that could lead to internal short circuits.

- **Autonomous Self-Healing**: Revolutionary damage recovery capability enables complete restoration of both ionic conductivity and mechanical properties within 60 seconds at 120°C. The healing is based on thermally-reversible aprotic Diels-Alder bonds that dissociate at elevated temperature and reform upon cooling, enabling multiple healing cycles without performance degradation. In practical battery operation, cells with damaged electrolyte recover 95% of their capacity after in-situ healing, extending battery lifetime and enabling damage-tolerant battery designs.

- **High Mechanical Strength**: Storage modulus of 11.3 MPa at 80°C surpasses most reported GPEs (typically <5 MPa), providing sufficient mechanical strength to suppress lithium dendrite penetration. The modulus remains 7.4 MPa even at the healing temperature (120°C), ensuring dimensional stability during the healing process.

- **Superior Solvent Retention**: 450% electrolyte uptake capacity with 95% solvent retention after 24 h at 80°C, significantly outperforming conventional GPEs. This exceptional solvent retention prevents performance degradation during long-term cycling and enables stable operation at elevated temperatures.

## Mechanism Analysis and Characterization

**Molecular Structure and Dynamic Bonding**: FTIR and Raman spectroscopy confirmed successful polymerization of PEGMA and incorporation of Q8M8 and 4-AAPM. Temperature-dependent FTIR (30-120°C) demonstrated reversible dissociation/reformation of D-A adducts between furan and maleimide groups, with characteristic furan C=O peak shifting from 1705 cm⁻¹ (associated) to 1720 cm⁻¹ (free) upon heating. Solid-state ¹³C NMR verified formation of D-A cycloadducts through characteristic chemical shifts at δ = 52.5 ppm (bridgehead carbon). XPS Si 2p spectrum (102.3 eV) confirmed Si-O-Si network formation from Q8M8 hydrolysis and condensation.

**Morphology and Phase Structure**: TEM and AFM revealed nanoscale phase separation with polysiloxane-rich domains (5-15 nm) uniformly dispersed in the PEGMA matrix. SAXS analysis showed correlation peak at q = 0.45 nm⁻¹, corresponding to domain spacing of ~14 nm. The nanostructure provides mechanical reinforcement while maintaining continuous ion transport pathways. SEM cross-sectional imaging demonstrated uniform electrolyte thickness (~80 μm) with excellent interfacial contact between electrodes and electrolyte.

**Ion Transport Mechanism**: PFG-NMR (pulsed-field gradient NMR) showed Li⁺ diffusion coefficient of 3.2 × 10⁻⁸ cm² s⁻¹ at 30°C, consistent with ionic conductivity measurements. MD simulations revealed Li⁺ transport primarily through PEGMA chains coordinated with ether oxygen atoms, with average Li-O coordination number of 4.2. The polysiloxane domains do not participate in Li⁺ conduction but enhance mechanical properties through nanoreinforcement. Activation energy for Li⁺ conduction calculated from Arrhenius plot: 0.18 eV (30-60°C) and 0.35 eV (-20 to 30°C), indicating two distinct conduction mechanisms.

**Mechanical Properties**: DMA (dynamic mechanical analysis) showed storage modulus of 11.3 MPa at 80°C with tan δ peak at 45°C (Tg of PEGMA phase). Tensile testing demonstrated stress at break of 2.8 MPa and elongation at break of 180% before healing, recovering to 2.7 MPa and 175% after healing (96.5% recovery). Scratch healing monitored by optical microscopy showed complete gap closure within 60 s at 120°C.

**Self-Healing Kinetics**: In-situ FTIR monitoring of healing process showed 80% D-A bond recovery within 30 s, reaching 95% after 60 s at 120°C. Reversible healing over 5 cycles demonstrated consistent recovery efficiency (>95% each cycle). The activation energy for D-A bond reformation calculated from temperature-dependent healing kinetics: 65.4 kJ mol⁻¹.

**Interfacial Analysis**: XPS depth profiling of Li metal anode after cycling revealed LiF (F 1s: 685.0 eV), Li₂CO₃ (C 1s: 289.8 eV), and organic species (C 1s: 286.5 eV) in SEI layer, with SEI thickness of ~45 nm measured by cross-sectional TEM. TOF-SIMS mapping showed uniform SEI composition, indicating homogeneous Li⁺ flux. Cryo-SEM of cycled Li anode demonstrated dense, dendrite-free morphology with average roughness (Ra) of 0.35 μm compared to 2.8 μm for liquid electrolyte control.

**Electrochemical Stability**: Linear sweep voltammetry (LSV) on stainless steel||Li cell showed oxidation onset at 4.7 V vs. Li/Li⁺. TGA-DSC confirmed thermal stability up to 350°C with decomposition onset at 380°C. Flame test demonstrated self-extinguishing behavior within 2 seconds, with UL-94 V-0 rating achieved.

## Conclusions and Significance

This research presents a groundbreaking plastic ceramic gel polymer electrolyte (GPE) that simultaneously achieves the "holy trinity" of electrolyte properties: high ionic conductivity (10⁻⁴ S cm⁻¹ scale), high mechanical modulus (>10 MPa), and autonomous self-healing capability. The innovative design integrates three key structural elements: (1) a PEGMA-based ion-conducting network providing high Li⁺ conductivity through flexible polymer chains with ethylene oxide coordination sites; (2) a polysiloxane (Q8M8) inorganic network creating ceramic-like mechanical reinforcement through nanoscale phase separation; and (3) a dynamic Diels-Alder covalent network enabling thermally-reversible crosslinking for damage repair.

The fundamental significance lies in overcoming the intrinsic trade-off between ionic conductivity and mechanical strength that has long limited GPE development. By decoupling mechanical reinforcement (polysiloxane domains) from ion transport pathways (PEGMA channels), the electrolyte achieves unprecedented property combination - conductivity comparable to liquid electrolytes with mechanical strength approaching solid-state electrolytes. More importantly, the aprotic dynamic covalent chemistry enables fully reversible healing without interfering with Li⁺ transport or electrochemical stability, a critical advancement over previously reported self-healing electrolytes that rely on proton-transfer mechanisms incompatible with battery operation.

Practical demonstrations using LFP||Li, NCM811||Li, and Li||Li cells showed exceptional cycling stability (96.8% retention after 1000 cycles for LFP||Li), wide-temperature operation (-20 to 60°C), and complete performance recovery after mechanical damage - capabilities rarely achieved simultaneously in single electrolyte systems. The non-flammability, excellent solvent retention, and simple fabrication process further enhance practical applicability.

For the battery field, this work establishes a new design paradigm for multifunctional electrolytes that address multiple failure modes simultaneously: mechanical damage, lithium dendrite penetration, thermal instability, and interfacial degradation. The self-healing capability introduces the concept of "damage-tolerant" or "repairable" batteries that could significantly extend lifetime and improve safety, particularly for applications where physical damage is unavoidable (electric vehicles, wearable electronics, aerospace). The hybrid organic-inorganic approach provides a generalizable strategy applicable beyond PEGMA system to other polymer matrices and inorganic fillers.

From application perspective, the electrolyte's compatibility with high-voltage cathodes (NCM811), operation across wide temperature range, and simple in-situ polymerization compatible with existing battery manufacturing processes position it as a promising candidate for next-generation high-energy-density lithium metal batteries. The demonstrated ability to heal damage in assembled cells without disassembly suggests potential for developing batteries with extended service lifetime and reduced maintenance requirements, addressing critical needs for sustainable energy storage technologies.
