## Research Background and Motivation

This research addresses the critical challenge of lithium dendrite growth and unstable solid electrolyte interphase (SEI) formation in lithium metal batteries, which significantly hinders their practical application. Despite theoretical advantages, lithium metal anodes suffer from:
- Uncontrollable lithium dendrite growth during cycling
- Formation of fragile and unstable SEI layers
- Consumption of active lithium and electrolyte
- Safety hazards from dendrite-induced short circuits

The research motivation stems from the need to construct a robust, multifunctional SEI layer that can:
- Suppress lithium dendrite growth
- Accommodate volume changes during cycling
- Maintain high ionic conductivity
- Enable low negative-to-positive (N/P) ratio operation for practical high-energy-density batteries

Traditional approaches using artificial SEI layers or electrolyte additives often suffer from insufficient mechanical strength, poor interfacial contact, or complex fabrication processes.

## Research Methods and Innovation Points

### Composition of Gel Electrolyte

| Component | Specific Material | Content/Ratio |
|-----------|------------------|---------------|
| **Polymer Matrix/Monomer** | Diethylene glycol diacrylate (DEGDA) | 3.5 wt% |
| **Lithium Salt** | LiTFSI (Lithium bis(trifluoromethanesulfonyl)imide) | 1.2 M |
| **Solvent** | Mixture of Fluoroethylene carbonate (FEC) and Ethyl methyl carbonate (EMC) | FEC:EMC = 3:7 by weight |
| **Additive** | FEC (also serves as solvent) | 30 wt% of liquid components |
| **Initiator/Crosslinking Agent** | Azobisisobutyronitrile (AIBN) | 0.5 wt% of monomer |
| **Polymerization Conditions** | Thermal initiation at 80°C for 30 min in Ar atmosphere | |

### Polymerization Method

- **Polymerization Type**: In-situ radical polymerization (thermal initiation)
- **Polymerization Conditions**: 80°C for 30 minutes in argon atmosphere, initiated by AIBN
- **Mechanism**: DEGDA monomers crosslink to form a 3D network polymer, while FEC solvent partially participates in polymerization through ring-opening polymerization

### Key Innovation Points

1. **Multifunctional SEI Construction**: The in-situ formed polymer network constructs a protective layer on lithium metal anode surface consisting of three regions: (1) dense polymer network near Li anode, (2) transitional layer with LiF-containing SEI components, and (3) liquid electrolyte-rich region near cathode

2. **Cathode Compatibility**: The polymer electrolyte penetrates the cathode structure, forming a continuous ion transport network that improves cathode wettability and maintains stable contact during cycling

3. **Low N/P Ratio Achievement**: The robust SEI layer enables stable cycling at extremely low N/P ratios (1.67) with high Coulombic efficiency, significantly reducing excess lithium requirements for practical applications

4. **Synergistic SEI Composition**: The SEI layer contains both organic (polymer) and inorganic (LiF, Li2CO3) components, providing both mechanical flexibility and high interfacial energy to suppress dendrite growth

### Special Performance

- **Wide-Temperature Performance**: The polymer electrolyte maintains high ionic conductivity (4.5 × 10⁻⁴ S cm⁻¹) at -40°C, enabling low-temperature operation
- **Safety**: FEC component provides flame retardant properties, improving battery safety characteristics

- **High Areal Loading**: Achieves stable cycling with high areal capacity loading (16.5 mg cm⁻² cathode, ~2.7 mAh cm⁻²), approaching practical application requirements

## Mechanism Analysis and Characterization

The research employed comprehensive characterization methods to reveal the working mechanism:

**1. SEI Composition Analysis (XPS Depth Profiling)**
- XPS analysis revealed a gradient SEI structure with three distinct regions:
  - Inner layer: Rich in organic polymer components (C-O, C=O bonds from DEGDA polymer)
  - Middle layer: Mixed inorganic-organic composition with significant LiF and Li2CO3 content
  - Outer layer: Higher concentration of LiF species from FEC decomposition
- The LiF-rich interface provides high interfacial energy to suppress lithium dendrite penetration

**2. Morphological Evolution (SEM and TEM)**
- SEM showed that the cycled lithium anode maintained smooth, dendrite-free morphology even after extended cycling
- Cross-sectional TEM confirmed the formation of a uniform, compact SEI layer with thickness ~40 nm
- EDS mapping revealed homogeneous distribution of F, C, and O elements throughout the SEI layer

**3. Cathode-Electrolyte Interface (Raman and Confocal Microscopy)**
- Raman spectroscopy confirmed the successful polymerization of DEGDA monomer
- Confocal fluorescence microscopy demonstrated uniform penetration of the polymer electrolyte into the cathode porous structure
- The polymer network maintains intimate contact with cathode particles during cycling, preventing delamination

**4. Mechanical Properties (Rheology)**
- Storage modulus measurements showed the polymer gel has appropriate mechanical strength (~8.3 × 10⁵ Pa)
- The modulus is sufficient to suppress dendrite growth while maintaining good interfacial contact

**5. Ion Transport Mechanism**
- The Li⁺ transference number of 0.53 indicates improved Li⁺ transport compared to conventional liquid electrolytes
- The 3D polymer network provides continuous ion transport pathways while restricting anion mobility

**6. Dendrite Suppression Mechanism**
- The multifunctional SEI layer provides:
  - Mechanical barrier to physically block dendrite penetration
  - High interfacial energy (from LiF-rich interface) to thermodynamically suppress dendrite nucleation
  - Uniform Li⁺ flux distribution to prevent localized deposition

**7. Cathode Compatibility**
- The polymer electrolyte wets the cathode surface effectively, forming a continuous ion-conducting network
- In-situ polymerization ensures conformal coating on cathode particles
- The interface remains stable during cycling, as evidenced by stable charge transfer resistance from EIS measurements

## Conclusions and Significance

This research successfully demonstrates a multifunctional solid electrolyte interface constructed via in-situ polymerization for lithium metal batteries. The core conclusions are:

**1. SEI Engineering Achievement**
- The in-situ polymerized DEGDA/FEC-based electrolyte forms a robust, multifunctional SEI layer on lithium metal anodes
- The SEI layer effectively suppresses lithium dendrite growth, enabling stable long-term cycling over 1000 hours in symmetric cells

**2. Practical Application Breakthrough**
- Demonstrated stable operation at extremely low N/P ratios (1.67, even 1.33), which is essential for practical high-energy-density lithium metal batteries
- Achieved stable cycling with high areal loading (16.5 mg cm⁻²), meeting requirements for practical battery applications
- Maintained excellent performance over 500 cycles in NCM811||Li full batteries

**3. Mechanistic Understanding**
- Revealed the gradient SEI structure combining organic polymer flexibility and inorganic LiF mechanical strength
- Demonstrated the synergistic effects of polymer network and SEI components in suppressing dendrite growth

**4. Significance for Battery Development**
- This approach addresses critical bottlenecks in lithium metal battery commercialization: dendrite growth, unstable SEI, and excessive lithium requirements
- The in-situ polymerization method is simple and scalable, with potential for industrial application
- The strategy provides a general framework for designing multifunctional interfaces for next-generation high-energy-density batteries

**5. Broader Impact**
- The methodology can be extended to other metal anode systems (Na, K, etc.) facing similar dendrite challenges
- The work demonstrates the power of combining polymer chemistry with interfacial engineering to solve fundamental battery challenges
- Provides valuable insights for designing future solid-state or quasi-solid-state battery systems

This research represents significant progress toward practical lithium metal batteries, offering a scalable solution to the long-standing challenges of lithium dendrite growth and interfacial instability.
