## Research Background and Motivation

Silicon is an attractive anode material for next-generation lithium-ion batteries due to its high theoretical specific capacity (~4200 mAh g⁻¹). However, the severe volume expansion (>300%) during lithiation/delithiation processes causes structural degradation and unstable solid-electrolyte interphase (SEI) formation. Existing solutions focus on nanostructuring and composite electrode design, but the large volume change remains a fundamental challenge that is difficult to address from the electrode structure alone. This research proposes a revolutionary approach: using a supremely elastic gel polymer electrolyte (GPE) as both ionic conductor and mechanical buffer to maintain electrode structural integrity during cycling, providing a new paradigm for designing high-performance lithium metal batteries.

## Research Methods and Innovation Points

### Composition of Gel Electrolyte

| Component | Specific Material | Content/Ratio |
|---------|---------|----------|
| **Polymer Matrix/Monomer** | Polyrotaxane (PR) with PEG and α-CD | PEG molecular weight: 35 kDa; α-CD threading ratio: ~40% |
| **Lithium Salt** | LiTFSI | 1 M concentration in liquid electrolyte |
| **Solvent** | Liquid electrolyte solution (EC/DEC with LiTFSI) | EC/DEC (1:1 v/v) with 1 M LiTFSI |
| **Additive** | None | - |
| **Initiator/Crosslinking Agent** | None (physical crosslinking via α-CD crystallites) | - |
| **Polymerization Conditions** | Physical gelation at room temperature | Self-assembly without chemical crosslinking |

### Polymerization Method

- **Polymerization Type**: Physical Gelation (No chemical polymerization required)
- **Polymerization Conditions**: Room temperature self-assembly through host-guest interactions between α-cyclodextrin (α-CD) and polyethylene glycol (PEG) chains, forming physically crosslinked polyrotaxane network

### Key Innovation Points

1. **Supreme Elasticity**: The polyrotaxane-based GPE exhibits unprecedented stretchability (up to 15,000% strain) and toughness (8,900 J m⁻²), far exceeding conventional polymer electrolytes, enabling it to accommodate silicon electrode volume expansion
2. **Sliding Crosslinking Mechanism**: The unique "slide-ring" polyrotaxane structure with movable α-CD crystallites along PEG chains provides reversible physical crosslinks that dissipate mechanical energy while maintaining structural integrity
3. **Dynamic Electrode-Electrolyte Interface**: The highly elastic GPE maintains continuous contact with the expanding/contracting silicon electrode, preventing SEI delamination and stabilizing the interface during cycling
4. **Dual-Function Design**: Serves simultaneously as ionic conductor and mechanical buffer, eliminating the need for separate electrolyte and binder components

### Special Performance

- **Wide-Temperature Performance**: The GPE maintains high ionic conductivity (2.5 × 10⁻³ S cm⁻¹ at 25°C) and stable cycling performance at temperatures as low as -20°C, demonstrating good low-temperature operation capability
- **Safety**: The gel polymer electrolyte exhibits self-extinguishing behavior and non-flammable characteristics, significantly improving battery safety compared to flammable liquid electrolytes
- **Mechanical Durability**: The unprecedented elasticity enables the GPE to withstand extreme deformation without fracture, maintaining electrical and mechanical integrity even after multiple stretching cycles
- **Interface Stability**: In situ optical microscopy reveals that the elastic GPE maintains continuous contact with the Si electrode during lithiation/delithiation, preventing electrode pulverization and SEI delamination

## Mechanism Analysis and Characterization

**Microscopic Characterization:**
- **In Situ Optical Microscopy**: Direct observation confirms that the elastic GPE maintains continuous contact with the expanding Si electrode during lithiation, preventing electrode delamination and crack formation that occurs with rigid electrolytes
- **Scanning Electron Microscopy (SEM)**: Cross-sectional images show preserved electrode morphology after 500 cycles with GPE, while liquid electrolyte cells show severe electrode pulverization and delamination
- **X-ray Photoelectron Spectroscopy (XPS)**: Depth profiling analysis demonstrates formation of stable and thin SEI layer (~30 nm) on Si anode with GPE, compared to thick and unstable SEI (>100 nm) with liquid electrolyte

**Spectroscopic Analysis:**
- **Fourier Transform Infrared (FTIR) Spectroscopy**: Confirms successful formation of polyrotaxane structure through characteristic peaks of α-CD and PEG, and reveals host-guest interactions between cyclodextrin cavities and PEG chains
- **Raman Spectroscopy**: Indicates no chemical degradation of the GPE after extended cycling, demonstrating electrochemical stability

**Mechanical Characterization:**
- **Tensile Testing**: Demonstrates exceptional stretchability (15,000% strain) and toughness (8,900 J m⁻²), with stress-strain curves showing characteristic elastomeric behavior
- **Dynamic Mechanical Analysis (DMA)**: Reveals low storage modulus (~13 kPa) and high loss factor, indicating soft and viscoelastic nature ideal for accommodating volume changes
- **Cyclic Tensile Testing**: Shows excellent recovery after multiple stretching cycles, confirming reversible elastic deformation

**Electrochemical Analysis:**
- **Electrochemical Impedance Spectroscopy (EIS)**: Demonstrates stable interfacial resistance over 500 cycles with GPE (<50 Ω), while liquid electrolyte shows rapidly increasing resistance (>300 Ω after 200 cycles)
- **Cyclic Voltammetry (CV)**: Reveals stable lithiation/delithiation peaks with minimal peak shift over 200 cycles, indicating highly reversible electrochemical reactions

**Molecular Mechanism:**
The unique polyrotaxane structure consists of α-CD rings threaded onto PEG chains, where the α-CD crystallites act as physical crosslinking points. Unlike conventional covalently crosslinked networks, these slide-ring crosslinks can move along the PEG backbone, allowing the network to dissipate mechanical energy through reversible dissociation and reassociation of α-CD crystallites. This sliding mechanism provides the supreme elasticity and toughness that enable the GPE to accommodate extreme volume changes of Si electrodes while maintaining ionic conduction pathways.

## Conclusions and Significance

This research demonstrates a revolutionary approach to addressing the fundamental challenge of silicon anode volume expansion by developing a supremely elastic polyrotaxane-based gel polymer electrolyte. The key achievements are:

1. **Unprecedented Mechanical Properties**: The GPE exhibits record-breaking elasticity (15,000% strain) and toughness (8,900 J m⁻²), surpassing all previously reported polymer electrolytes by orders of magnitude
2. **Stable Silicon Anode Cycling**: Enabled 500-cycle operation of Si-C composite anodes with 78% capacity retention, representing a significant improvement over liquid electrolyte systems
3. **Mechanistic Insight**: Established the correlation between electrolyte elasticity and electrode structural stability, providing design principles for next-generation battery electrolytes
4. **Practical Demonstration**: Successful full-cell operation with NCM811 cathode demonstrates the technology's potential for commercial applications

**Scientific Significance**: This work shifts the paradigm from modifying electrode structures to engineering electrolyte mechanical properties, opening new avenues for designing high-energy-density batteries with volume-changing electrode materials. The slide-ring polyrotaxane concept represents a fundamental innovation in polymer electrolyte design, with broader implications for energy storage systems beyond lithium-ion batteries.

**Application Value**: The elastic GPE technology addresses critical bottlenecks in silicon anode commercialization and could accelerate the development of high-energy-density lithium-ion batteries for electric vehicles and grid-scale energy storage. The non-flammable nature also improves battery safety, addressing a key concern for large-scale applications.
