## Research Background and Motivation

Lithium metal batteries (LMBs) are considered the "Holy Grail" of energy storage systems due to their ultrahigh theoretical energy density. However, two critical safety issues have hindered their practical application:
1. **Dendritic lithium growth** during repeated plating/stripping cycles, which can pierce separators and cause internal short circuits
2. **Flammability of liquid electrolytes**, leading to potential fires and explosions

While traditional strategies focus on modifying anodes or using expensive solid electrolytes, this research aims to develop a safe, cost-effective, and high-performance gel polymer electrolyte (GPE) through **in situ gelation** of traditional liquid electrolytes. The approach maintains the high ionic conductivity of liquid electrolytes while enhancing safety and dendrite suppression capabilities.

## Research Methods and Innovation Points

### Composition of Gel Electrolyte

| Component | Specific Material | Content/Ratio |
|---------|---------|----------|
| **Polymer Matrix/Monomer** | Acrylamide (AM) | 8 wt% relative to liquid electrolyte |
| **Lithium Salt** | LiTFSI | 1 M concentration in liquid electrolyte |
| **Solvent** | Fluoroethylene carbonate (FEC) and dimethyl carbonate (DMC) | 1:1 by volume |
| **Additive** | None specified |
| **Initiator/Crosslinking Agent** | Azobisisobutyronitrile (AIBN) | 0.5 wt% relative to AM monomer |
| **Polymerization Conditions** | Thermal initiation at 60°C for 2 hours under Ar atmosphere | In situ formation within battery |

### Polymerization Method

- **Polymerization Type**: In-situ radical polymerization
- **Polymerization Conditions**: 60°C, 2 hours, thermally initiated by AIBN, occurring within assembled battery
- **Key Feature**: Liquid electrolyte transforms into quasi-solid gel directly inside the battery device, ensuring perfect electrode-electrolyte contact

### Key Innovation Points

1. **In Situ Gelation Strategy**: Converts conventional liquid electrolyte into gel polymer electrolyte directly inside the battery after assembly, eliminating poor interfacial contact issues common with pre-formed GPEs
2. **Cost-Effective Approach**: Uses only 8 wt% monomer (acrylamide) to transform traditional electrolyte, much more economical than solid-state electrolytes
3. **Dual Function of PAM Matrix**: The polyacrylamide (PAM) framework simultaneously provides mechanical strength to suppress dendrites and maintains high ionic conductivity through plasticization by liquid electrolyte

### Special Performance

- **Wide-Temperature Performance**: Excellent performance from -40°C to 60°C. At -40°C, the GPE-based battery maintains 80 mAh g⁻¹ capacity (vs. only 20 mAh g⁻¹ for liquid electrolyte control). The high dielectric constant of PAM (ε = 45) enables effective Li⁺ desolvation and transport at low temperatures.
- **Safety**: The gel electrolyte is completely non-flammable. Direct flame exposure causes no combustion, and the gel maintains structural integrity. The plastic crystal transition of PAM around 60°C provides additional thermal stability.
- **High Specific Energy**: Demonstrated with high-voltage LiCoO2||Li batteries (4.2V) and high-energy-density LiFePO4||Li pouch cells achieving 325 Wh kg⁻¹ (based on cathode active material).
- **Self-Standing Property**: The freestanding nature of the PAM-LiTFSI/FEC-DMC gel electrolyte enables flexible battery design and easy manufacturing.

## Mechanism Analysis and Characterization

The research employed comprehensive characterization to elucidate the mechanism:

1. **Morphology Analysis (SEM)**: Revealed that PAM-LiTFSI/FEC-DMC GPE possesses a porous, three-dimensional network structure with pore sizes ranging from nanometers to micrometers. This structure efficiently accommodates liquid electrolyte while providing mechanical support.

2. **Interfacial Analysis**: Post-cycling SEM showed that lithium metal anodes cycled with GPE maintained smooth, dendrite-free surfaces, whereas those with liquid electrolyte developed extensive dendritic and mossy lithium deposits.

3. **Spectroscopic Analysis (FTIR and Raman)**: Confirmed successful polymerization of acrylamide and strong interaction between PAM carbonyl groups and Li⁺ ions. The P(AM-co-LiTFSI) copolymer forms effective Li⁺ transport pathways.

4. **Thermal Analysis (DSC and TGA)**: Demonstrated that the GPE has enhanced thermal stability with a plastic crystal transition around 60°C (characteristic of PAM) and no decomposition below 150°C.

5. **Electrochemical Impedance Spectroscopy (EIS)**: Showed that the GPE has lower interfacial resistance with lithium metal compared to liquid electrolyte, and the resistance remains stable during long-term cycling.

6. **Mechanism of Dendrite Suppression**: The shear modulus (~10⁴ Pa) of the GPE, though below the theoretical value required to mechanically suppress dendrites, combined with uniform Li⁺ flux distribution and stable SEI formation, effectively prevents dendrite penetration.

7. **Mechanism of Enhanced Safety**: The crosslinked PAM network physically traps flammable liquid components, preventing their release and combustion during thermal abuse.

## Conclusions and Significance

This study successfully demonstrates that **in situ gelation of traditional liquid electrolytes** represents a simple, cost-effective, and scalable strategy for upgrading lithium metal battery technology. The core conclusions are:

1. The PAM-based GPE achieves **high ionic conductivity** (1.1 × 10⁻³ S cm⁻¹) comparable to liquid electrolytes while providing **non-flammability** and **dendrite suppression** capabilities.

2. The **in situ formation** strategy ensures perfect electrode-electrolyte contact, eliminating interfacial issues that plague pre-formed gel and solid electrolytes.

3. Batteries incorporating this GPE demonstrate **excellent cycling stability** (500 cycles with 80% retention), **wide-temperature operability** (-40°C to 60°C), and **superior safety** (non-flammable).

4. The approach uses only **8 wt% polymer**, making it economically viable for large-scale applications unlike expensive solid-state electrolytes.

**Significance**: This work provides a practical pathway toward safe, high-energy-density lithium metal batteries without requiring complete replacement of existing liquid electrolyte manufacturing infrastructure. The in situ gelation concept can be extended to other polymer monomers and battery systems, offering broad applicability for next-generation energy storage technologies. The strategy effectively bridges the gap between high-performance liquid electrolytes and safe solid-state systems, accelerating the commercialization of lithium metal batteries for electric vehicles and grid storage applications.
