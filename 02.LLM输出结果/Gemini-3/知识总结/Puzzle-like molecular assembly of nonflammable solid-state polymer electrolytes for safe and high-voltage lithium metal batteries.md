## Research Background and Motivation

This research addresses two critical challenges in developing high-energy-density lithium metal batteries (LMBs): safety concerns associated with liquid electrolyte flammability and poor interfacial contact between solid-state electrolytes (SSEs) and electrodes. Conventional SSEs suffer from high interfacial resistance and poor Li⁺ transport across interfaces due to rigid electrode/electrolyte contact. The authors aimed to develop a flame-retardant solid-state polymer electrolyte (SPE) that could maintain intimate contact with electrodes during battery operation while achieving high ionic conductivity and high-voltage stability. The research was motivated by the need for solid-state batteries that can operate safely at high voltages (≥4.5 V) with high specific energy (>600 Wh kg⁻¹).

## Research Methods and Innovation Points

### Composition of Gel Electrolyte

| Component | Specific Material | Content/Ratio |
|---------|---------|----------|
| **Polymer Matrix/Monomer** | PEGMA (Poly(ethylene glycol) methyl ether methacrylate) | Oligomer matrix |
| **Crosslinking Agent** | TMPTA (Trimethylolpropane triacrylate) | 3 acrylate groups per molecule |
| **Lithium Salt** | LiTFSI (Lithium bis(trifluoromethanesulfonyl)imide) | Concentration optimized for ion transport |
| **Solvent** | Non-flammable ionic liquid analogue | Custom-designed flame-retardant molecules |
| **Additive** | Flame-retardant organic phosphonate | Integrated into molecular structure |
| **Initiator/Crosslinking Agent** | Thermal/chemical initiator | Enables in-situ polymerization |
| **Polymerization Conditions** | In-situ thermal or chemical initiation | Forms solid-state electrolyte within cell |

### Polymerization Method

- **Polymerization Type**: In-situ Crosslinking Radical Polymerization
- **Polymerization Conditions**: Thermal or chemical initiation at elevated temperature; oligomers (PEGMA with low molecular weight ~500) flow into electrode pores before crosslinking via TMPTA to form flexible solid-state polymer network

### Key Innovation Points

1. **Puzzle-like Molecular Assembly Design**: Utilization of low-molecular-weight PEGMA oligomers (Mn = 500) with TMPTA crosslinker that creates a "puzzle-like" interlocking structure with electrode materials, enabling rapid self-healing and continuous maintenance of intimate electrode/electrolyte contact during cycling
2. **Non-flammable Molecular Engineering**: Integration of flame-retardant organic phosphonate into the electrolyte structure, creating a solid-state polymer electrolyte with intrinsic flame retardancy (non-flammable characteristics) that is achieved without compromising ionic conductivity
3. **Decoupled Ion Transport and Mechanical Properties**: Achievement of high ionic conductivity (1.3 × 10⁻³ S cm⁻¹ at 25°C) through a molecular design that reduces crystallinity (low Tg ~ -58°C to -62°C) while maintaining sufficient mechanical strength for solid-state battery operation, overcoming the typical trade-off between conductivity and mechanical properties

### Special Performance

- **Wide-Temperature Performance**: The electrolyte maintains ionic conductivity of ~1.0 × 10⁻⁴ S cm⁻¹ at -40°C, enabling low-temperature operation. At 80°C, conductivity reaches 3.8 × 10⁻³ S cm⁻¹, indicating excellent thermal performance across wide temperature range (-40°C to 80°C)
- **Safety**: The SPE exhibits excellent flame retardancy with non-flammable characteristics demonstrated by direct flame testing. The integrated organic phosphonate structure provides intrinsic flame retardancy without external additives, significantly enhancing battery safety compared to conventional liquid electrolytes
- **Other Characteristic Performance**: The puzzle-like molecular assembly enables continuous self-healing and maintenance of intimate electrode/electrolyte contact during battery operation, as evidenced by XPS depth profiling showing unchanged chemical states after 200 cycles and EIS showing decreased interfacial resistance (from initial ~380 Ω cm² to ~150 Ω cm² after formation), resulting in a high specific energy of 606.4 Wh kg⁻¹ at the cell level

## Mechanism Analysis and Characterization

The research employed comprehensive characterization methods to elucidate the key mechanisms:

1. **Molecular Structure and Polymerization**: FTIR spectroscopy confirmed complete polymerization of acrylate groups (C=C peak at 1637 cm⁻¹ disappeared) and successful formation of the crosslinked network. Raman spectroscopy provided additional evidence of the molecular structure and polymerization.

2. **Thermal and Mechanical Properties**: DSC analysis revealed very low glass transition temperatures (-58°C to -62°C), indicating high chain mobility and decoupling of ion transport from mechanical constraints. DMA (Dynamic Mechanical Analysis) showed storage modulus of ~1.1 × 10⁶ Pa, providing sufficient mechanical integrity while maintaining flexibility.

3. **Interfacial Contact and Self-Healing**: Cross-sectional SEM images demonstrated that the low-viscosity oligomers (before polymerization) readily infiltrated electrode pores, and the resulting SPE maintained intimate contact after polymerization. The puzzle-like molecular assembly enabled continuous self-healing of micro-cracks and gaps formed during cycling. XPS depth profiling of cycled Li metal anodes revealed unchanged chemical states of C, O, and F species after 200 cycles, confirming stable interfacial contact. The interfacial resistance decreased from initial ~380 Ω cm² to ~150 Ω cm² after formation, indicating improved interfacial contact.

4. **High-Voltage Stability**: Linear sweep voltammetry (LSV) demonstrated electrochemical stability window up to 5.1 V (vs. Li⁺/Li). XPS analysis of cycled NCM811 cathodes showed stable CEI (cathode electrolyte interphase) formation with appropriate thickness, enabling stable high-voltage operation up to 4.6 V.

5. **Lithium Dendrite Suppression**: Post-mortem SEM analysis of Li metal anodes after symmetric cell cycling (1200 hours) showed smooth, dendrite-free morphology, indicating effective suppression of lithium dendrite growth. This is attributed to the intimate interfacial contact and uniform Li⁺ flux enabled by the puzzle-like molecular assembly.

6. **Flame Retardancy Mechanism**: The integrated organic phosphonate structure provides intrinsic flame retardancy through chemical mechanisms rather than physical additives. Direct flame testing showed the SPE does not ignite and self-extinguishes immediately, unlike conventional liquid electrolytes that burn continuously.

## Conclusions and Significance

This research demonstrates a successful puzzle-like molecular assembly approach to develop non-flammable solid-state polymer electrolytes that address the critical interfacial contact problem in solid-state batteries. The key conclusions and significance are:

1. **Fundamental Breakthrough**: The puzzle-like molecular assembly using low-Mₙ PEGMA oligomers and TMPTA crosslinker creates an interlocking structure that enables continuous self-healing and maintenance of intimate electrode/electrolyte contact, overcoming the major limitation of poor interfacial contact in conventional rigid SSEs.

2. **Safety Achievement**: The integration of flame-retardant organic phosphonate into the molecular structure creates intrinsically non-flammable SPEs, addressing the critical safety concern of battery flammability without compromising ionic conductivity.

3. **High-Voltage Capability**: The SPE achieves an electrochemical stability window of 5.1 V and demonstrates stable cycling of high-voltage cathodes (NCM811 at 4.6 V, LiCoO₂ at 4.6 V), enabling the development of high-energy-density LMBs.

4. **Practical Performance**: The NCM811||Li pouch cell achieves a high specific energy of 606.4 Wh kg⁻¹ with stable cycling (89.1% retention after 100 cycles at 0.5 C, 86.7% retention after 200 cycles), demonstrating practical viability for high-energy-density applications.

5. **Interfacial Stability**: The Li||Li symmetric cell shows exceptional stability for 1200 hours at 0.1 mA cm⁻² without short circuit, and the interfacial resistance decreases during cycling, indicating continuous improvement of interfacial contact.

6. **Wide Temperature Operation**: The SPE maintains good ionic conductivity from -40°C to 80°C (1.0 × 10⁻⁴ S cm⁻¹ to 3.8 × 10⁻³ S cm⁻¹), enabling operation across a wide temperature range.

7. **Scientific Impact**: This work provides a generalizable molecular design strategy for developing high-performance solid-state polymer electrolytes that simultaneously address multiple critical requirements: ionic conductivity, mechanical integrity, interfacial contact, flame retardancy, and high-voltage stability. The puzzle-like molecular assembly concept can be extended to other polymer electrolyte systems and applications.

8. **Application Prospects**: The demonstrated performance metrics (606.4 Wh kg⁻¹ specific energy, non-flammability, high-voltage stability, wide-temperature operation) make this SPE system highly promising for next-generation safe, high-energy-density solid-state lithium metal batteries for electric vehicles, portable electronics, and grid energy storage applications.
