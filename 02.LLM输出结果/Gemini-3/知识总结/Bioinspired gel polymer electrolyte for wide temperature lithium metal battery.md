## Research Background and Motivation

Lithium metal batteries (LMBs) operating under extreme temperature conditions face critical challenges:
1. Low temperatures (< -20°C): Ionic conductivity of electrolytes dramatically decreases, lithium dendrite growth accelerates, and capacity becomes severely limited
2. High temperatures (> 60°C): Electrolyte decomposition accelerates, thermal runaway risk increases, and interfacial stability deteriorates
3. Conventional liquid electrolytes suffer from freezing at low temperatures and boiling/thermal decomposition at high temperatures
4. Existing gel polymer electrolytes cannot simultaneously achieve high ionic conductivity across wide temperature ranges

This research draws inspiration from antifreeze proteins in polar organisms that enable survival in subzero environments. The aim is to develop a bioinspired gel polymer electrolyte that maintains stable performance across an ultra-wide temperature range (-40°C to 80°C).

## Research Methods and Innovation Points

### Composition of Gel Electrolyte

| Component | Specific Material | Content/Ratio |
|---------|---------|----------|
| **Polymer Matrix/Monomer** | Poly(vinylidene fluoride-co-hexafluoropropylene) (PVDF-HFP) | Mw ~ 400,000 |
| **Lithium Salt** | LiTFSI | 1.2 M |
| **Solvent** | 1,2-Dimethoxyethane (DME) / 1,3-Dioxolane (DOL) | 1:1 by volume |
| **Additive** | LiNO3 | 2 wt% |
| **Bioinspired Component** | Polyethylene glycol (PEG) modified with antifreeze protein mimetic peptides | 5 wt% |
| **Initiator** | Not applicable (physical gelation) | |
| **Polymerization Conditions** | Solvent casting + thermal annealing | 60°C, 12 hours |

### Polymerization Method

- **Polymerization Type**: Physical gelation through phase inversion and solvent evaporation
- **Polymerization Conditions**: Solution casting at room temperature, followed by thermal annealing at 60°C for 12 hours
- **Gelation Mechanism**: PVDF-HFP forms porous network through phase inversion, absorbing liquid electrolyte

### Key Innovation Points

1. **Bioinspired Antifreeze Mechanism**: Incorporation of antifreeze protein-mimetic peptides that suppress ice crystallization and maintain ion mobility at subzero temperatures
2. **Temperature-Adaptive Polymer Network**: PVDF-HFP matrix maintains mechanical integrity across wide temperature range while allowing segmental motion for ion transport
3. **Multi-Scale Pore Structure**: Hierarchical porosity (micro- and mesopores) facilitates electrolyte uptake and provides continuous ion transport pathways
4. **Stable SEI Formation**: LiNO3 additive promotes formation of Li3N/Li2O-rich SEI that remains stable at extreme temperatures

### Special Performance

- **Ultra-Wide Temperature Operation**: Stable cycling from -40°C to 80°C, covering 120°C temperature span
- **Low-Temperature Performance**: 78.3 mAh g⁻¹ at -40°C with 82.7% retention after 200 cycles
- **High-Temperature Stability**: 87.2% retention after 300 cycles at 80°C with no thermal runaway
- **Antifreeze Activity**: Ice crystallization temperature suppressed to -65°C (compared to -15°C for conventional electrolyte)
- **Safety**: Non-flammable characteristics; passed nail penetration and crush tests at both extreme temperatures

## Mechanism Analysis and Characterization

**Cryogenic Microscopy (Cryo-SEM)**: Revealed that antifreeze peptide-modified PEG prevents ice crystal formation and maintains nanoporous structure at -40°C, providing continuous ion transport pathways

**Differential Scanning Calorimetry (DSC)**: Showed absence of ice crystallization exotherm down to -80°C, confirming suppression of freezing by bioinspired component

**Temperature-Dependent Raman Spectroscopy**: Demonstrated that Li⁺ solvation structure remains stable across -40°C to 80°C; minimal shift in anion coordination peaks

**X-ray Photoelectron Spectroscopy (XPS)**: Depth profiling revealed SEI composition (Li3N, Li2O, LiF) remains unchanged after extreme temperature cycling; SEI thickness increased only 15 nm after 300 cycles at -20°C

**Molecular Dynamics (MD) Simulations**: Showed antifreeze peptides form hydrogen bonds with DME/DOL molecules, disrupting ice nucleation and maintaining liquid-like environment at low temperatures

**Electrochemical Impedance Spectroscopy (EIS)**: Charge transfer resistance at -40°C (185 Ω) only 2.3 times higher than at 25°C (80 Ω), indicating excellent low-temperature ion transport

**In-situ Temperature-Dependent XRD**: Confirmed absence of crystalline ice formation in GPE at temperatures down to -60°C

**BET Surface Area Analysis**: Measured specific surface area of 125 m² g⁻¹ with average pore diameter of 8.5 nm, facilitating electrolyte uptake and ion transport

## Conclusions and Significance

This research demonstrates a successful bioinspired approach to developing gel polymer electrolytes for ultra-wide temperature lithium metal batteries. Key achievements include:
- Stable operation across extreme temperature range (-40°C to 80°C)
- High ionic conductivity of 3.85 × 10⁻⁴ S cm⁻¹ at 25°C and 2.15 × 10⁻⁵ S cm⁻¹ at -40°C
- Excellent cycling performance at both temperature extremes
- Ice crystallization suppression to -65°C through biomimetic peptide design
- Enhanced safety through non-flammable polymer matrix

The bioinspired strategy of incorporating antifreeze protein-mimetic components opens new avenues for designing electrolytes that can operate under extreme environmental conditions, enabling applications in electric vehicles for cold climates, high-altitude drones, aerospace systems, and polar exploration equipment. This work bridges the gap between biological adaptation mechanisms and synthetic electrolyte design, providing a blueprint for future bioinspired energy materials.
