## Research Background and Motivation

High-energy lithium metal batteries (LMBs) are expected to play important roles in next-generation energy storage systems for electric vehicles, unmanned planes, and smart electrical grids. However, uncontrolled lithium dendrite growth and pulverization in conventional liquid electrolytes seriously impede LMBs from authentic commercialization. While gel polymer electrolytes (GPEs) and solid polymer electrolytes (SPEs) exhibit superior stability toward lithium anodes compared to traditional liquid electrolytes, interfacial problems remain, especially separation between cathode materials and electrolytes. Although in-situ polymerization has been proven effective for synthesizing GPEs/SPEs with compatible interfaces, most current strategies require extra non-electrolytic monomers, initiators, and special conditions like high temperature. There is a lack of productive strategies that can utilize conventional commercialized electrolyte materials without impurity introduction while being implementable under moderate external conditions. This research aims to develop a facile strategy to convert traditional ether-based electrolytes into quasi-solid GPEs without additives or harsh conditions, making large-scale commercialization of LMBs feasible.

## Research Methods and Innovation Points

### Composition of Gel Electrolyte

| Component | Specific Material | Content/Ratio |
|---------|---------|----------|
| **Polymer Matrix/Monomer** | 1,3-dioxolane (DOL) as monomer; 1,2-dimethoxyethane (DME) as liquid plasticizer; Polydioxolane (PDXL) as polymer framework | DOL:DME = 1:1 (v/v) in precursor; 91.0% polymerization conversion rate of DOL |
| **Lithium Salt** | LiPF₆ (serves dual role as salt and initiator reservoir); LiTFSI | 2 M LiPF₆ in precursor solution; base electrolyte: 1 M LiTFSI in DOL/DME |
| **Solvent** | DOL (polymerizes to PDXL); DME (remains as liquid phase) | DOL/DME = 1:1 (v/v) initially |
| **Additive** | Trace H₂O (12 ppm) necessary for polymerization termination | 12 ppm |
| **Initiator/Crosslinking Agent** | LiPF₆ decomposition releases PF₅ (Lewis acid initiator); H⁺(PF₅OH)⁻ formed with trace H₂O | Generated in-situ from LiPF₆ |
| **Polymerization Conditions** | Ambient temperature (room temperature); Standing time: ~10 hours for complete gelation; Dissolution heat of LiPF₆ promotes partial decomposition | Temperature: Room temperature; Time: ~10 h |

### Polymerization Method

- **Polymerization Type**: Cationic ring-opening polymerization of DOL induced by LiPF₆
- **Polymerization Conditions**: Room temperature (ambient conditions); standing for ~10 hours; LiPF₆ dissolution heat promotes partial decomposition releasing PF₅ gas (detected by GC-MS); PF₅ combines with trace H₂O (12 ppm) to form H⁺(PF₅OH)⁻ initiator; protonation of DOL monomers forms reactive oxonium ions; repetitious DOL insertion leads to polymer chain growth; H₂O attacks oxonium ions for chain termination via nucleophilic substitution

### Key Innovation Points

1. **Additive-Free In-Situ Gelation Strategy**: First demonstration of using commercial LiPF₆ to induce cationic ring-opening polymerization of DOL at room temperature without external monomers, initiators, or heating, representing one of the easiest methods to fabricate polymer-based electrolytes with incomparable advantages including commercialized materials, freedom from impurities, and moderate conditions
2. **Dual-Function LiPF₆ Mechanism**: LiPF₆ serves simultaneously as lithium salt and "initiator reservoir" - its dissolution heat promotes partial decomposition releasing PF₅ gas, which combines with trace H₂O to form H⁺(PF₅OH)⁻ initiator enabling spontaneous polymerization under ambient conditions, achieving high molecular weight PDXL (Mn ~52,000) with 91% conversion rate
3. **Polymer Framework-Liquid Plasticizer Combination**: Unique quasi-solid structure combining high molecular weight linear-chain PDXL framework (with ether bond-rich structure for Li⁺ conduction) and liquid DME plasticizer, maintaining high ionic conductivity (3.8×10⁻³ S cm⁻¹), low volatilization (1/6 of liquid electrolyte), superior dendrite suppression, broadened electrochemical window (up to 4.6 V), and universal compatibility with diverse cathodes (sulfur, LiFePO₄, NCM622)

### Special Performance

- **Dendrite Suppression**: SEM morphology after cycling at 1.0 mA cm⁻² showed smooth Li surface with few sporadic coarse sites and no dendrites or pulverization layer for GPE system; in contrast, LE system exhibited predominantly pulverized rough surface with typical protruding dendrite structures and pulverization layer eroded >50 μm deep into Li foil
- **SEI Stability**: Li-Cu battery tests showed GPE achieved 90% initial Coulombic efficiency at 1 mA cm⁻² reaching >95% after several cycles and remaining stable for 100 cycles without decline; LE control showed lower initial efficiency (81%) with integral decline after 20 cycles due to repetitive SEI breakage/repair; overpotential remained stable for GPE (no polarization increment) vs. dramatic increase from 30 to 70 mV (10th to 50th cycle) for LE
- **Polysulfide Shuttling Suppression**: Permeation test demonstrated polysulfides scarcely infiltrated GPE even after 48 hours while LE showed no resistance; separator from GPE-based Li-S battery remained nearly original color after 50 cycles at 0.5 C while LE-based separator turned yellow; impedance remained stable for GPE vs. continuous resistance increase for LE due to insulated sulfide deposits on anode
- **Self-Discharge Resistance**: Li-S battery with GPE showed only 6% capacity decay after 1-week interruption at highest polysulfide concentration (2.05 V cutoff), with minor capacity rise in subsequent cycles indicating reversible activation contribution; LE-based battery showed 40% irreversible capacity loss under same conditions
- **High-Voltage Stability**: LSV demonstrated LE decomposition starting at 4.0 V with serious decomposition >4.2 V; GPE showed no obvious decomposition current until 4.6 V; transformation of unstable cyclic DOL into stable linear-chain PDXL broadened electrochemical stability window enabling NCM622 compatibility at 4.3 V cutoff
- **Mechanical Flexibility**: Soft-packed NCM622||Li battery with GPE exhibited no power delivery failure under various mechanical deformations including bending and folding, attributed to reduced GPE mobility and higher mechanical strength maintaining stronger interfacial attachment
- **Low Volatilization**: Weight change monitoring showed GPE volatilization only 1/6 that of pristine liquid electrolyte at room temperature

## Mechanism Analysis and Characterization

**Cationic Ring-Opening Polymerization Mechanism**: The gelation chemistry represents the first application of LiPF₆-induced cationic polymerization in lithium secondary battery systems. GC-MS analysis detected gaseous PF₅ released from partial LiPF₆ decomposition promoted by its dissolution heat at the relatively high concentration (2 M) used. PF₅ acts as a strong Lewis acid initiator combining with trace H₂O (12 ppm) to form H⁺(PF₅OH)⁻, which induces DOL monomers to convert into reactive oxonium ions via fast protonation. Repetitious interposition of DOL monomers into oxonium ions leads to polymer chain growth. Chain termination occurs when trace H₂O attacks oxonium ions via nucleophilic substitution. XPS spectrum detected small amounts of LiF generated from LiPF₆ decomposition, though too little to be visible macroscopically.

**Structural Characterization of PDXL**: ¹H NMR spectrum showed chemical shifts at 3.72 ppm (assigned to H on –O–CH₂–CH₂–O–) and 4.75 ppm (–O–CH₂–O–) with integral area ratio 2:1, confirming –CH₂–O–CH₂–CH₂–O– as the repeating unit. ¹³C NMR and FTIR spectra corroborated this structure. The purified PDXL achieved molecular weight Mn ~52,000 (based on polystyrene standard) after 10 hours polymerization, with remarkably high conversion rate of 91.0% from monomer DOL to polymer.

**Gelation Process Evolution**: Real-time monitoring during polymerization showed molecular weight (Mn) and ionic conductivity changing in opposite trends, consistent with cationic polymerization characteristics. Initially high ionic conductivity decreased as polymer chains grew. After 10 hours, Mn reached ~52,000 and ionic conductivity stabilized at 3.8×10⁻³ S cm⁻¹. The high conductivity results from the unique strategy of transforming only part of liquid phase (DOL) into solid framework (PDXL), while DME liquid phase acts as plasticizer combining with PDXL. The ether bond-rich linear chain PDXL structure exhibits prominent Li⁺ delivery ability similar to polyethylene oxide. DME liquid phase tremendously reduces transport resistance within GPE.

**Li Dendrite Suppression Mechanism**: Symmetrical Li||Li cell cycling at 0.5 and 1.0 mA cm⁻² demonstrated GPE maintains remarkably low overpotential (~20-25 mV at 0.5 mA cm⁻², stable at 1.0 mA cm⁻²) for >800 and >400 hours respectively, while LE showed continuously increasing polarization. Post-cycling SEM revealed smooth Li surface without dendrites for GPE vs. predominantly pulverized surface with protruding dendrite structures and >50 μm deep pulverization layer for LE. The improved interfacial compatibility arises from polymer framework confinement of liquid phase, guaranteeing less contact between Li surface and reactive electrolyte components, effectively reducing interfacial reactions and promoting even Li⁺ dispersion on Li surface.

**Polysulfide Confinement in Li-S Batteries**: In-situ polymerization achieved sufficient precursor infiltration into sulfur electrode, with SEM showing originally exposed cathode and separator evenly covered by GPE both on surface and inside cathode. Polymer framework acts as physical blockage for polysulfide migration, confirmed by permeation test showing polysulfides scarcely infiltrated GPE after 48 hours vs. no resistance in LE. Post-cycling separator analysis (50 cycles at 0.5 C) showed nearly original color for GPE vs. yellow for LE, evidencing polysulfides sealed within cathode chamber. CV profiles of GPE overlapped well with typical sulfur redox peaks (anodic: 2.30 and 2.40 V; cathodic: 2.25 and 2.05 V) without side reactions, while LE showed visible oxidation peak shift indicating detrimental polarization from shuttle effect. XPS characterization of cycled Li anodes revealed feather-like sulfide deposits from polysulfide-Li side reactions in LE system blocking normal Li⁺ plating/stripping, while GPE system showed smooth deposit-free surface.

**High-Voltage Electrochemical Stability**: The instability of liquid DOL/DME originates from DOL's unstable cyclic structure, becoming unstable at 4.0 V with serious decomposition >4.2 V. Ring-opening polymerization stabilizes cyclic DOL into long-chain linear PDXL structure, improving electrolyte tolerance toward high-voltage electrochemical processes. LSV demonstrated no obvious GPE decomposition current until 4.6 V, enabling normalized NCM622 cycling at 4.3 V cutoff without oxidation-led overcharging phenomenon that plagued LE systems.

## Conclusions and Significance

This work presents a groundbreaking strategy to upgrade traditional liquid electrolytes into quasi-solid gel polymer electrolytes via in-situ gelation at ambient temperature without external additives, initiators, or heating. The innovative approach exploits the intrinsic chemistry of commercial LiPF₆ to induce cationic ring-opening polymerization of DOL from conventional ether-based electrolytes, representing one of the most facile and straightforward methods to fabricate polymer-based electrolytes. The dual-function mechanism where LiPF₆ serves simultaneously as lithium salt and initiator reservoir (releasing PF₅ through dissolution heat-promoted decomposition) enables spontaneous polymerization under room temperature, achieving high molecular weight PDXL (Mn ~52,000) with exceptional 91% conversion rate.

The resulting GPE exhibits a unique quasi-solid structure combining PDXL polymer framework with liquid DME plasticizer, delivering superior performance across multiple metrics: high ionic conductivity (3.8×10⁻³ S cm⁻¹), low volatilization (1/6 of liquid electrolyte), effective dendrite suppression (smooth Li surface without pulverization after >800 hours cycling), and broadened electrochemical window (4.6 V enabling high-voltage cathode compatibility). The remarkable universality across diverse cathode chemistries demonstrates practical viability: Li-S batteries achieved outstanding 97% Coulombic efficiency without additives, 73.7% capacity retention after 500 cycles (741 mAh g⁻¹) and 50% retention after 1000 cycles with only 0.05% decay per cycle; LiFePO₄||Li batteries maintained 95.6% capacity retention after 700 cycles with >99.5% Coulombic efficiency; NCM622||Li batteries realized normalized cycling at 4.3 V cutoff breaking voltage limitations of traditional ether-based electrolytes.

The significance extends beyond performance metrics to practical implementation feasibility. The strategy utilizes exactly the most conventional commercialized electrolyte materials (DOL/DME/LiPF₆/LiTFSI) without impurity introduction, demonstrates unprecedented adaptability to current battery packing technologies (including flexible soft-packed cells maintaining power delivery under mechanical deformation), and requires no special equipment or conditions for manufacturing. The polymer framework physically confines polysulfides eliminating shuttle effect, maintains stable SEI layers preventing repetitive breakage/repair, and provides superior self-discharge resistance (only 6% capacity decay vs. 40% for liquid electrolyte after 1-week interruption).

This work establishes a new paradigm for upgrading traditional electrolyte systems through innovative chemistry rather than complicated engineering, opening a new frontier in advanced electrolyte development for future lithium metal batteries. The demonstrated compatibility with industrial-scale production processes, combined with superior safety (reduced volatilization, dendrite suppression) and performance across multiple cathode systems, positions this technology for near-term commercialization in high-energy applications including electric vehicles, unmanned aerial vehicles, and smart electrical grids requiring both high energy density and long-term reliability.
