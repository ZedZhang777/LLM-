## Research Background and Motivation

Solid-state lithium metal batteries (SSLMBs) offer high energy densities and improved safety compared to conventional lithium-ion batteries. However, their commercialization is hindered by several critical challenges: limited ionic conductivity, large interfacial resistance between solid electrolytes and electrodes, uncontrollable lithium dendrite growth, and continuous polysulfide shuttling in Li-S batteries. Traditional poly(ethylene oxide) (PEO)-based solid polymer electrolytes (SPEs) suffer from high crystallinity, poor mechanical strength, and inadequate electrochemical stability. The flexibility of all-solid-state polymer electrolytes cannot fully solve interfacial contact problems due to interfacial ion transfer barriers between the electrolyte and uneven electrodes. While in-situ polymerization can address interface issues, it is limited to few monomer types with uncontrollable polymerization degrees. This research aims to develop a self-healing solid polymer electrolyte that can dynamically repair interfacial defects throughout the battery's life cycle, thereby achieving stable solid-solid interfacial contact and enabling high-performance solid-state Li-S batteries.

## Research Methods and Innovation Points

### Composition of Gel Electrolyte

| Component | Specific Material | Content/Ratio |
|---------|---------|----------|
| **Polymer Matrix/Monomer** | Polytetrahydrofuran (PTMG, Mw=2000 g mol⁻¹), Hexamethylene diisocyanate (HDI), 2-Hydroxyethyl disulfide (BHDS) | PTMG:HDI:BHDS = 1:2.4:1 (molar ratio) |
| **Lithium Salt** | Lithium bis(fluorosulfonyl)imide (LiFSI) | Polymer:LiFSI = 2:1 (mass ratio) |
| **Solvent** | Dimethylacetamide (DMAc), Dichloromethane (CH₂Cl₂) | Removed by vacuum drying |
| **Additive** | N/A | N/A |
| **Initiator/Crosslinking Agent** | Dibutyltin dilaurate (DBTDL) as catalyst | 5 μL |
| **Polymerization Conditions** | Temperature: 40°C; Time: 12 h total (1 h for PTMG-HDI, then BHDS addition); Final vacuum drying: 65°C for 48 h |

### Polymerization Method

- **Polymerization Type**: Step-growth polymerization (condensation polymerization of polyalcohol and polyisocyanate)
- **Polymerization Conditions**: 40°C in N₂-filled glove box for initial reaction; DMAc added every 2 hours (3 times); final vacuum drying at 65°C for 48 h to form solid film

### Key Innovation Points

1. **Dynamic Dual-Bond Self-Healing Mechanism**: Introduction of both dynamic covalent disulfide bonds (S-S) with low bond dissociation energy and extensive hydrogen bonding networks between urethane groups (-NH-COO-), enabling rapid interfacial self-healing at room temperature without external stimulation
2. **Dual-Integrated Electrode/Electrolyte Strategy**: Novel fabrication approach where polymer electrolyte precursor solutions are cast directly onto both cathode and anode surfaces, creating integrated SPAN@SPE and Li@SPE structures that weld together during assembly, dramatically reducing interfacial resistance
3. **Optimized Molecular Design**: Use of PTMG soft segments with abundant ether-oxygen groups for Li⁺ solvation and conduction, combined with urethane groups for mechanical strength and hydrogen bonding, achieving superior balance of ionic conductivity (2.4×10⁻⁴ S cm⁻¹ at 30°C), mechanical properties (88.3 MPa strength, 2000% elongation), and electrochemical stability (>5.0 V)

### Special Performance

- **Self-Healing Capability**: Complete interfacial self-healing at 30°C within 1 hour; self-healed film maintains mechanical strength close to original value; enabled by dynamic disulfide bond rearrangement and hydrogen bond reformation
- **Interfacial Contact Quality**: Ultrasound imaging demonstrated superior interfacial contact in integrated structure (PPV increased from 0.57 to 0.60 V during cycling) compared to laminated structure (PPV decreased from 0.11 to 0.06 V), with color change from green to yellow indicating progressive interface improvement
- **SEI Layer Composition**: High inorganic Li-F content (94%) and low organic C-F content (6%) in SEI layer, indicating stable LiFSI decomposition and suppressed electrolyte decomposition, contributing to enhanced cycling stability
- **Rate Performance**: Excellent rate capability with capacity retention from 0.1 C (641 mAh g⁻¹) to 1.0 C (557 mAh g⁻¹) for SPAN cathode; capacity recovery upon returning to 0.1 C
- **Wide Voltage Compatibility**: Electrochemical stability window exceeding 5.0 V vs Li/Li⁺, enabling compatibility with high-voltage cathode materials

## Mechanism Analysis and Characterization

**Self-Healing Mechanism**: ATR-FTIR and XPS confirmed the presence of dynamic disulfide bonds (~635 cm⁻¹) and hydrogen bonds (C=O peak splitting at 1719 and 1680 cm⁻¹). ¹H NMR verified successful incorporation of BHDS chain extender through characteristic peaks at 4.08 and 2.94 ppm (1:1 ratio for -S-CH₂-CH₂-S-). The low bond dissociation energy of S-S bonds enables dynamic rearrangement at room temperature, while the extensive hydrogen bond network between urethane groups provides additional self-healing pathways.

**Interfacial Evolution**: In-situ ultrasound imaging technology revealed real-time interfacial contact during battery operation. For integrated cells, the peak-peak amplitude value (PPV) increased from 0.57 to 0.60 V over 10 cycles, indicating progressive interface improvement through self-healing. In contrast, laminated cells showed decreasing PPV (0.11 to 0.06 V) and expanding blue regions, indicating poor contact and gas generation.

**Li⁺ Transport Mechanism**: Raman spectroscopy demonstrated 95.2% free FSI⁻ anions in PTMG-HDI-BHDS/LiFSI (vs. 68.5% in PTMG-HDI/LiFSI), indicating superior lithium salt dissociation. DFT calculations showed PTMG segments have higher LUMO energy (1.35 eV vs. 0.64 eV for PEO) and stronger Li⁺ binding energy (ΔE = -0.83 eV vs. -0.75 eV), facilitating Li⁺ solvation and transport. The high Li⁺ transference number (0.81) results from hydrogen bonding between urethane groups and FSI⁻ anions (confirmed by upfield shift in ¹⁹F NMR), which restricts anion mobility.

**SEI Layer Characterization**: Depth-profiled XPS (Ar⁺ sputtering 0-200 s) revealed a LiF-rich SEI layer (94% Li-F, 6% C-F before etching) on the Li anode with PTMG-HDI-BHDS/LiFSI, compared to organic-rich SEI (72% C-F, 28% Li-F) with PEO/LiFSI. The inorganic-rich SEI provides excellent electronic insulation while enabling rapid Li⁺ migration, stabilizing the Li metal interface.

**Structural Characterization**: Cross-sectional SEM and EDS mapping of pouch cells confirmed complete integration of SPAN@SPE and Li@SPE layers without delamination, with uniform distribution of S and F elements indicating well-formed 3D interpenetrating Li⁺ channels. XRD analysis showed significantly reduced crystallinity in PTMG-HDI-BHDS compared to PEO, improving Li⁺ mobility. GPC monitoring revealed molecular weight growth to 7.3×10⁵ g mol⁻¹ with viscosity of 1.3×10⁴ cps after 8 h polymerization.

## Conclusions and Significance

This work presents a breakthrough poly(ether-urethane)-based solid polymer electrolyte featuring dual self-healing mechanisms through dynamic covalent disulfide bonds and hydrogen bonding networks. The innovative dual-integrated electrode/electrolyte fabrication strategy, validated by in-situ ultrasound imaging, achieves exceptional solid-solid interfacial contact and eliminates traditional interfacial resistance problems. The resulting PTMG-HDI-BHDS/LiFSI electrolyte demonstrates an unprecedented combination of properties: high ionic conductivity (2.4×10⁻⁴ S cm⁻¹ at 30°C), exceptional Li⁺ transference number (0.81), wide electrochemical window (>5.0 V), and superior mechanical strength (88.3 MPa with 2000% elongation). 

The Li||Li symmetric cells achieved stable cycling for over 6000 hours at 0.2 mA cm⁻², while solid-state Li-S batteries delivered outstanding performance: SPAN cathodes maintained 93% capacity retention after 700 cycles at 0.3 C (560 mAh g⁻¹), and S@CB cathodes achieved 812 mAh g⁻¹ after 350 cycles with 84.4% retention. These results significantly surpass most reported solid-state Li-S batteries and demonstrate practical feasibility through pouch cell validation.

The significance of this research extends beyond performance metrics. The self-healing polymer design principle addresses the fundamental challenge of dynamic interfacial contact in all-solid-state batteries, offering a scalable solution (kilogram-level synthesis demonstrated) for next-generation energy storage systems. The ultrasound imaging methodology provides a powerful diagnostic tool for real-time interface monitoring in solid-state batteries. This dual-integrated strategy opens new avenues for developing high-energy-density, long-life solid-state batteries with practical applications in electric vehicles and grid-scale energy storage.
