## Research Background and Motivation

Solid-state polymer electrolytes (SPEs) offer promising solutions for lithium metal batteries (LMBs) by addressing safety concerns related to liquid electrolyte leakage and flammability. However, polycarbonate-based polymer electrolytes face critical challenges including low ionic conductivity at 25°C, low Li⁺ transference number (tLi+), poor high-voltage performance, instability with lithium metal anodes (LMAs), and high flammability. While poly(ethylene oxide) (PEO)-based electrolytes have been extensively studied, unmodified polycarbonate-based solid-state electrolytes remain flammable despite mitigating liquid leakage risks. Various strategies including incorporating inorganic fillers, blending organic materials, editing molecular structures, and forming gel electrolytes have been proposed, but they fail to simultaneously achieve excellent lithium salt dissociation capability, high tLi+, stable LMA performance, high-voltage stability, and superior flame retardancy. The research aims to develop a facile and scalable preparation strategy to enhance the overall performance of polycarbonate-based solid-state electrolytes for high-specific-energy lithium metal batteries.

## Research Methods and Innovation Points

### Composition of Gel Electrolyte

| Component | Specific Material | Content/Ratio |
|---------|---------|----------|
| **Polymer Matrix/Monomer** | Vinyl ethylene carbonate (VEC), 2,2,3,3,4,4,4-heptafluorobutyl methacrylate (HFBMA), Triallyl phosphate (TAP) | n(VEC):n(HFBMA):n(TAP) = 1:0.1:0.15 (molar ratio) |
| **Lithium Salt** | Lithium bis(trifluoromethanesulfonyl)imide (LiTFSI) | 20 wt% (optimal, relative to total monomer mass) |
| **Solvent** | N/A (solvent-free after polymerization) | Removed during polymerization |
| **Additive** | N/A | N/A |
| **Initiator/Crosslinking Agent** | Azobisisobutyronitrile (AIBN) | 1.0 wt% (relative to sum of VEC, HFBMA, and TAP) |
| **Polymerization Conditions** | Pre-polymerization: 65°C for 2 h; Final polymerization: 70°C for 6 h; Al₂O₃-coated PE separator used as scaffold | Temperature: 65-70°C; Time: 8 h total |

### Polymerization Method

- **Polymerization Type**: In-situ free radical polymerization
- **Polymerization Conditions**: Pre-polymerization at 65°C for 2 hours to form sticky prepolymer solution; infiltration into PE separators followed by final polymerization at 70°C for 6 hours; thermally initiated by AIBN

### Key Innovation Points

1. **Puzzle-like Molecular Assembly Strategy**: Novel approach combining three distinct molecular fragments (VEC for Li⁺ conduction, F-rich HFBMA for high-voltage stability and robust interphase formation, P-rich TAP for flame retardancy and high-voltage stability) through facile in-situ free radical polymerization to create multifunctional SPE
2. **Enhanced Anion Restriction Mechanism**: Introduction of F- and P-containing segments increases anion affinity and restricts TFSI⁻ migration, achieving exceptionally high Li⁺ transference number (0.70) while maintaining high ionic conductivity (0.432 mS cm⁻¹ at 25°C) through preferential TFSI⁻ binding to polymer backbone
3. **Dual-Layer Interphase Engineering**: Designed molecular structure enables formation of inorganic-rich (LiF-dominant) inner SEI/CEI layers with thin organic outer layers through preferential reduction/oxidation of HFBMA and TAP segments, providing superior electrochemical stability window (5.15 V) and excellent compatibility with both LMAs and high-voltage cathodes

### Special Performance

- **Wide-Temperature Performance**: Excellent high-temperature stability demonstrated by Li||LFP cell cycling stably for over 120 cycles at 100°C, while PVEC-based cell failed after only 2 cycles
- **Safety Performance**: Superior non-flammability confirmed by direct flame tests showing no ignition or burning; pouch cells passed nail penetration tests without thermal runaway, smoke, fire, or combustion; minimal surface temperature increase during nail penetration; heating plate experiments showed uniform temperature distribution without swelling
- **Accelerating Rate Calorimetry (ARC)**: Higher thermal stability temperatures compared to commercial liquid electrolyte - T1 (onset of thermal instability): 160.8°C vs. 139.7°C; T2 (thermal runaway trigger): 244.4°C vs. 188.9°C (55.5°C higher); demonstrates superior safety under thermal abuse conditions
- **High-Voltage Stability**: Electrochemical floating test showed stable low leakage current (~5 μA) at 4.9 V, while PVEC exhibited dramatic current increase at 4.5 V
- **Mechanical Abuse Tolerance**: Pouch cells continued powering LED devices under repeated twisting and cutting without liquid leakage, sparks, or explosions
- **Interfacial Stability**: Dual-layered SEI structure with inorganic-rich inner layer (94% LiF in inner SEI vs. 28% for PVEC) and thin organic outer layer; uniform and thin CEI (~2 nm) on NCM622 vs. thick uneven CEI (~7 nm) with PVEC

## Mechanism Analysis and Characterization

**Li⁺ Transport and Salt Dissociation Mechanism**: Molecular dynamics (MD) simulations revealed distinct coordination environments in IWSWN-SPE system. Li⁺ coordinates not only with C=O groups on the polymer backbone and VEC monomers but also with P=O groups on TAP fragments, providing multiple Li⁺ transport pathways. Lower binding energy between IWSWN-SPE and Li⁺ (-3.40 eV) compared to PVEC (-3.75 eV) facilitates easier Li⁺ hopping between coordination sites, reducing energy barrier for Li⁺ conduction. Radial distribution function (RDF) analysis showed weaker Li⁺-TFSI⁻ association in IWSWN-SPE (CN(Li-OTFSI⁻) = 0.21) compared to PVEC (0.41), indicating superior salt dissociation. Raman spectroscopy confirmed 95.2% free FSI⁻ anions in IWSWN-SPE vs. 68.5% in PVEC. ⁷Li ssNMR showed chemical shift from -0.60 ppm (LiTFSI) to -0.45 ppm (IWSWN-SPE), confirming increased Li⁺ mobility. DFT calculations revealed significantly lower LiTFSI dissociation energy in IWSWN-SPE (0.76 eV) compared to PVEC (2.97 eV).

**Anion Restriction Mechanism**: MD simulations demonstrated stronger TFSI⁻ interactions with IWSWN-SPE polymer chains. TFSI⁻ diffusion coefficient in IWSWN-SPE (3.24×10⁻⁸ cm² s⁻¹) was 2.67 times lower than in PVEC (8.64×10⁻⁸ cm² s⁻¹), while Li⁺ diffusion coefficients remained similar (3.51×10⁻⁸ vs. 3.73×10⁻⁸ cm² s⁻¹). Structural snapshots showed reduced N-O distances between TFSI⁻ and polymer chains in IWSWN-SPE (3.98 Å and 4.06 Å) compared to PVEC (4.42 Å and 5.16 Å). Binding energy calculations confirmed stronger IWSWN-SPE-TFSI⁻ interaction (-2.11 eV) vs. PVEC-TFSI⁻ (-1.51 eV). Electrostatic potential distribution showed positive regions on HCF-H in HFBMA segments providing anion-binding sites, while negative regions on O=C=O (VEC) and O=P=O (TAP) interact with Li⁺.

**High-Voltage Stability Mechanism**: DFT calculations revealed IWSWN-SPE exhibits lowest HOMO energy level (-8.04 eV) indicating strong oxidative stability, with HOMO orbital distribution primarily on HFBMA and TAP moieties. LUMO energy level (-0.45 eV) showed improved reductive stability compared to VEC (-0.83 eV), with LUMO localized on HFBMA fragments enabling preferential reduction for F-rich SEI formation.

**SEI Characterization**: Cryo-TEM revealed dual-layered SEI structure with continuous inorganic-rich inner layer (primarily LiF with Li₂CO₃, LiOH, LiPO₃, Li₂O) and thin amorphous outer layer. FFT patterns confirmed LiF (111) and (200) planes. TOF-SIMS depth profiling showed LiF₂⁻ distributed throughout SEI layer in IWSWN-SPE, while concentrated near surface in PVEC. Organic species (C₂HO⁻, CH⁻) concentrated in outer layer with lower total content than PVEC. XPS depth profiling (0, 20, 100 s etching) revealed organic C content in IWSWN-SPE (42.3%, 52.1%, 53.4%) significantly lower than PVEC (58.4%, 66.4%, 66.2%). LiF content after 20/100 s etching: 15.3%/14.4% (IWSWN-SPE) vs. 9.23%/7.07% (PVEC). C-F species from HFBMA and LiPO₃ from TAP detected in SEI. SEM showed compact, homogeneous surface and dense cross-section for cycled Li in IWSWN-SPE vs. uneven surface with porous, mossy-like aggregations in PVEC.

**CEI Characterization**: TEM revealed thin uniform CEI (~2 nm) on NCM622 with IWSWN-SPE vs. thick uneven CEI (~7 nm) with PVEC. NCM622 cycled in IWSWN-SPE maintained excellent structural integrity without cracks and retained layered structure with smaller rock-salt transition layer. XPS showed higher LiF content in CEI from IWSWN-SPE (>22.0% in inner layers) vs. PVEC (7.8%). Organic C content in inner CEI layer: 29.4% (IWSWN-SPE) vs. 65.1% (PVEC). Stronger C-F signals from HFBMA participation. Weaker lattice O signal (529.7 eV) for IWSWN-SPE indicated complete CEI coverage preventing transition metal dissolution. TOF-SIMS confirmed lower C₂HO⁻ and higher uniform LiF₂⁻ distribution, with PO₂⁻/PO₃⁻ species present. Lower NiF₃⁻ signal indicated suppressed TM dissolution.

## Conclusions and Significance

This work presents a breakthrough puzzle-like molecular assembly strategy to construct high-performance solid-state polymer electrolytes through in-situ free radical polymerization of three functional molecular segments. The developed IWSWN-SPE successfully addresses multiple critical challenges simultaneously: achieving high ionic conductivity (0.432 mS cm⁻¹ at 25°C), exceptionally high Li⁺ transference number (0.70), wide electrochemical stability window (5.15 V), superior non-flammability, and excellent compatibility with both lithium metal anodes and high-voltage cathodes.

The molecular-level design enables synergistic effects: VEC segments provide Li⁺ conduction pathways, F-rich HFBMA segments enhance high-voltage stability and form robust fluorinated interphases, while P-rich TAP segments impart flame retardancy and contribute to inorganic-rich interphase formation. This results in outstanding battery performance including Li||Li symmetric cells cycling stably for over 2500 hours at 0.2 mA cm⁻², Li||NCM622 cells maintaining 73.5% capacity retention after 300 cycles with capability to operate with high mass loading (15.8 mg cm⁻²), and Li||LFP cells cycling stably for 120 cycles at extreme temperature (100°C).

The significance extends to practical applications demonstrated through 2.6-Ah Li||NCM811 pouch cells achieving 349 Wh kg⁻¹ specific energy. Superior safety performance validated by ARC testing (thermal runaway trigger temperature 55.5°C higher than liquid electrolyte) and successful nail penetration tests without thermal runaway provides compelling evidence for real-world viability. The facile and scalable in-situ polymerization approach is compatible with existing liquid-based battery manufacturing systems, offering a practical pathway for large-scale production of safe, high-energy-density solid-state lithium metal batteries. This puzzle-like molecular assembly strategy opens new possibilities for customizing advanced polymer electrolyte systems through rational combination of functional molecular fragments, advancing the development of next-generation energy storage technologies for electric vehicles and grid-scale applications.
