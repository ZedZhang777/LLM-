## Research Background and Motivation

Silicon-based materials are promising anodes for next-generation lithium-ion batteries due to their high specific capacities (3860 mAh g⁻¹) and low operation potentials. However, the huge volume expansion and shrinkage during lithiation/delithiation cycles (up to 300%) result in severe displacement of silicon particles, structural collapse of electrodes, unstable solid-electrolyte interphase (SEI) layers, and electrode peeling-off from current collectors. This leads to rapid capacity fade and represents a major obstacle hindering silicon-based anode technology commercialization. While various structured silicon materials, silicon-carbon composites, and functional binders have been developed to address particle pulverization and maintain electrode integrity, the displacement of material particles and structural collapse at the electrode level remain unsolved, especially at high mass loadings. Although polyrotaxanes-based polymers and self-healing polymers can repair damage after cracking occurs, strategies to intrinsically alleviate particle displacement and electrode cracking before damage happens are highly needed. This research aims to develop a supremely elastic gel polymer electrolyte serving as an intra-electrode cushion to reduce electrode expansion during lithiation and facilitate structure recovery during delithiation.

## Research Methods and Innovation Points

### Composition of Gel Electrolyte

| Component | Specific Material | Content/Ratio |
|---------|---------|----------|
| **Polymer Matrix/Monomer** | Poly(tetramethylene ether) glycol (PTMG, Mw=2900) as soft domain; 4,4'-methylene diphenyl diisocyanate (MDI) and ethylenediamine (EDA) as hard domain | Optimal ratio - PTMG:MDI:EDA = 1:2:1 (molar ratio); Copolymer 1 formulation |
| **Lithium Salt** | LiPF₆ (absorbed from liquid electrolyte) | 1 M (in absorbed electrolyte) |
| **Solvent** | Dimethylacetamide (DMAc) for synthesis; EC/DEC (1:1 v/v) absorbed for GPE formation | 48 wt% electrolyte uptake; 10 wt% FEC additive |
| **Additive** | Fluoroethylene carbonate (FEC) | 10 wt% in absorbed electrolyte |
| **Initiator/Crosslinking Agent** | N/A (step-growth polymerization) | N/A |
| **Polymerization Conditions** | Two-step synthesis: Step 1 at 80°C for 4 h (MDI+PTMG); Step 2 at 70°C for 4 h (addition of EDA); Final drying at 60°C | Temperature: 70-80°C; Time: 8 h total |

### Polymerization Method

- **Polymerization Type**: Step-growth polymerization (polyurethane formation via reaction between isocyanate and hydroxyl/amine groups)
- **Polymerization Conditions**: First step: MDI and PTMG heated at 80°C for 4 hours under inert argon to form isocyanate-capped PTMG intermediate; Second step: EDA added at room temperature, then heated to 70°C for 4 hours; Polymer obtained by evaporating solvent at 60°C; Application: polymer solution (5 wt% in DMAc) cast onto SiO electrode, self-infused, dried at room temperature overnight, then 120°C for 2 hours under vacuum

### Key Innovation Points

1. **Soft-Hard Domain Combination Strategy**: Novel copolymer design combining soft ether-based domain (PTMG) for electrolyte swelling and hard cyclic ring-based domain (MDI-EDA) for preventing excessive swelling and providing chemical weak interactions (hydrogen bonding and π-π interactions), resulting in supreme elasticity (>2300% elongation for GPE, Young's modulus 0.7 MPa) while maintaining structural integrity
2. **Intra-Electrode Cushion Concept**: GPE serves as uniformly distributed elastic cushion within electrode to reduce absolute particle displacement and electrode expansion during lithiation (53% thickness increase vs. 94% for control), and facilitate structural recovery during delithiation (120% final thickness vs. 152% for control), intrinsically preventing electrode cracking rather than repairing damage after occurrence
3. **SEI Modification Mechanism**: Elastic GPE participates in SEI formation, reducing inorganic salt decomposition products (LiF, Li₂CO₂R, LixPOyFz) and increasing organic components concentration, resulting in more stable interfacial chemistry with superior adhesion strength (0.4 N vs. 0.2 N for bare electrode) and minimal impedance growth during cycling

### Special Performance

- **Electrode Structural Stability**: In-situ thickness measurement showed significantly reduced electrode expansion - 53% thickness increase during lithiation (vs. 94% for control) and 120% final thickness after delithiation (vs. 152% for control); electrode maintained stable thickness changes in subsequent cycles indicating reliable structure without severe cracking
- **Morphological Integrity**: SEM imaging revealed much fewer and smaller cracks after 10-20 cycles for GPE-incorporated electrode compared to large cracks and electrode delamination in control samples; cross-sectional images showed dense electrode structure vs. large internal cracks causing current collector delamination in control
- **Interfacial Stability**: XPS depth profiling showed GPE-containing electrode had lower concentration of inorganic Li salts in SEI (LixPOyFz, LiF, Li₂CO₂R) and higher organic component concentration, indicating GPE participation in SEI formation reduced electrolyte decomposition; stable impedance without clear increase over 100 cycles vs. rapid resistance growth in control
- **High Coulombic Efficiency**: Average Coulombic efficiency of 99.9% achieved over 300 cycles in full cells, meeting critical threshold for practical Si-based anodes (calculated requirement: 99.9% CE needed for 80% capacity retention after 225 cycles)
- **Strong Adhesion**: Peel-adhesion test showed 2× higher adhesion strength (0.4 N vs. 0.2 N); electrode remained intact after vigorous stirring in solvent while control electrode suffered severe particle peeling
- **Comparison with Other Polymers**: Elastic GPE outperformed conventional polymers (PAA, PVA, PAA-PVA, PVdF-HFP) which showed poor elasticity, low ionic conductivity, reduced cycling stability, and large polarization

## Mechanism Analysis and Characterization

**Structural Design and Elasticity Mechanism**: The copolymer's supreme elasticity originates from the unique soft-hard domain architecture. FTIR spectroscopy confirmed polyurethane formation through characteristic O=C-N signals. The soft PTMG domain (Mw=2900) provides flexibility and swells in carbonate electrolytes, while the hard MDI-EDA cyclic ring domain prevents excessive swelling through hydrogen bonding and π-π interactions within polymer chains. Stress-strain testing revealed the GPE (after electrolyte uptake) exhibited superior mechanical properties compared to dry copolymer - tolerating >2300% elongation (vs. >900% for dry polymer) with Young's modulus of 0.7 MPa (vs. 1.2 MPa dry). The optimal MDI-EDA:PTMG ratio of 2:1 balanced mechanical strength (modulus 1.2 MPa) with electrolyte absorption capacity (48 wt% uptake).

**Electrode-Level Structural Protection**: In-situ thickness monitoring during lithiation/delithiation revealed the elastic GPE mechanism. During initial lithiation, both electrodes showed no thickness change initially due to electrode porosity accommodating particle expansion. As lithiation progressed, the control SiO electrode exhibited 94% thickness increase, while GPE-incorporated electrode showed only 53% increase - the elastic GPE cushion compressed to accommodate particle expansion. During delithiation, the GPE-electrode recovered to 120% of original thickness (attributed to SEI, inactive Li₂O, lithium silicate formation), while control remained at 152% due to severe irreversible cracking. This indicates the elastic GPE intrinsically reduces particle displacement and facilitates structural recovery.

**Interfacial Chemistry Modification**: High-resolution XPS characterization of cycled electrodes revealed distinct SEI compositions. The GPE-incorporated electrode showed characteristic O=C-N signals (399.7 eV in N 1s, 287.6 eV in C 1s) from the copolymer, indicating GPE participation in SEI formation. Quantitative analysis showed markedly lower inorganic salt concentrations - reduced LixPOyFz (686.2 eV in F 1s), LiF (684.8 eV in F 1s), Li₂CO₂R (287.7 eV in C 1s), and CH₂OCOOLi (288.9 eV in C 1s) compared to control. Higher organic component concentrations (C-C/C-H at 284.6 eV, C-O at 286.0 eV) indicated reduced electrolyte decomposition. Cross-sectional EDS mapping confirmed uniform N element distribution throughout electrode, verifying uniform GPE distribution.

**Morphological Evolution**: SEM imaging documented dramatic differences in electrode integrity. Control electrodes developed large surface cracks after only 10 cycles, with cross-sectional images revealing extensive internal cracking causing electrode delamination from copper current collectors. These cracks grew larger after 20 cycles. In contrast, GPE-incorporated electrodes maintained dense, crack-free morphology even after 20 cycles with minimal surface defects. Electrochemical impedance spectroscopy corroborated these findings - GPE cells showed stable resistance over 100 cycles while control samples exhibited rapid resistance growth, consistent with progressive electrode degradation.

**Adhesion and Structural Integrity**: Peel-adhesion testing quantified interfacial stability - GPE-incorporated electrodes exhibited 0.4 N adhesion strength, double that of bare electrodes (0.2 N). Vigorous stirring tests demonstrated superior particle retention, with GPE electrodes remaining intact while control samples suffered severe particle loss. XPS analysis revealed uniform polymer coating on SiO particles, and cross-sectional EDS showed homogeneous N element distribution, confirming the GPE functions as a uniformly distributed intra-electrode cushion rather than just a surface coating.

## Conclusions and Significance

This work demonstrates a breakthrough strategy using a supremely elastic gel polymer electrolyte to intrinsically alleviate the structural collapse of silicon-based anodes at the electrode level. The key innovation lies in the rational design of a copolymer combining soft ether-based domains (PTMG) with hard cyclic ring-based domains (MDI-EDA), achieving exceptional elasticity (>2300% elongation) while maintaining high ionic conductivity (2.4×10⁻⁴ S cm⁻¹ at 25°C). This elastic GPE serves as an intra-electrode cushion that fundamentally changes the mechanical behavior during cycling - reducing electrode thickness expansion from 94% to 53% during lithiation and enabling structural recovery to 120% (vs. 152% for control) during delithiation.

The practical significance is demonstrated through remarkable electrochemical performance: SiO anodes achieved stable capacity of 1068 mAh g⁻¹ over 250 cycles in half cells, and SiO||NCM523 full cells delivered 70% capacity retention over 350 cycles with commercial-level reversible capacity of 3.0 mAh cm⁻² and critically important average Coulombic efficiency of 99.9%. This meets the calculated requirement (99.9% CE for 80% retention after 225 cycles) for practical Si-based anode deployment.

The mechanism studies reveal that the elastic GPE not only mechanically cushions particle displacement but also participates in SEI formation, creating a more stable organic-rich interfacial layer with reduced electrolyte decomposition. The dramatically improved electrode integrity (minimal cracking, strong adhesion, stable impedance) addresses the long-standing problem of electrode-level structural collapse that has plagued high-loading silicon anodes.

This approach represents a paradigm shift from post-damage repair strategies to preventive structural stabilization, offering a scalable solution compatible with commercial electrode manufacturing (solution casting method). The work provides a foundation for designing functional gel polymer electrolytes for practical high-performance silicon-based anodes in next-generation lithium-ion batteries, with potential applications in electric vehicles and grid energy storage requiring high energy density and long cycle life. The demonstrated compatibility with commercial-level mass loadings (4.7 mg cm⁻²) and industry-relevant performance metrics positions this technology for near-term practical implementation.
