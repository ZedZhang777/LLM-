## Research Background and Motivation

Development of stable solid-electrolyte interphases (SEI) represents fundamental challenge for Li-metal batteries. Although fluorinated reagents improve cycling performance through LiF-rich SEI formation, their associated costs and environmental impacts remain problematic. Current research predominantly centers on fluorinated interfaces, with emerging fluorine-free polymer electrolyte systems mainly employing borate salts with traditional matrices (PMMA, PEO) or nano-composite PMMA-based electrolytes as primary components. However, relatively narrow electrochemical window severely restricts compatibility with high-voltage positive electrodes, and lack of in-depth research on SEI composition/structure results in poor cycling performance. Previous fluorine-reduction strategies (fluorine-free solvents, reduced fluorine content, fluorine-free additives) still rely on forming LiF-rich interfaces. Current fluorine-free systems face challenges: (1) Limited ionic conductivity; (2) Inability to form stable anion-rich SEI with controlled composition; (3) Poor cycling stability with high-nickel high-voltage cathodes. Achievement of fully fluorine-free SEI with highly stable Li metal batteries has yet to be addressed, urgently requiring development of fluorine-free polymer electrolyte with high interfacial compatibility through molecular structure design and interface engineering optimization.

## Research Methods and Innovation Points

### Composition of Gel Electrolyte

| Component | Specific Material | Content/Ratio |
|---------|---------|----------|
| **Polymer Matrix/Monomer** | VC (vinylene carbonate), MBA (N,N'-methylenebisacrylamide) | VC + MBA at 2% molar ratio MBA to VC |
| **Lithium Salt** | LiBOB (lithium bis(oxalate)borate), LiNO₃ (lithium nitrate) | 1.0 mol L⁻¹ LiBOB + 0.2 mol L⁻¹ LiNO₃ |
| **Solvent** | PC (propylene carbonate), DME (1,2-dimethoxyethane) | PC:DME = 1:3 by volume (base electrolyte PDE) |
| **Additive** | LiNO₃ serves as interfacial film-forming additive | 0.2 mol L⁻¹ |
| **Initiator/Crosslinking Agent** | AIBN (azobisisobutyronitrile) initiator; MBA as crosslinker | 0.5 wt% AIBN; MBA provides hydrogen bonding crosslinking |
| **Polymerization Conditions** | 70°C for 6 h under argon atmosphere; 70 µL precursor on glass fiber separator | In-situ radical copolymerization forming PVM (poly(vinylene carbonate-co-N,N'-methylenebisacrylamide)) |

**Note**: Final electrolyte is PVM-GPE = PVM polymer matrix + 1 M LiBOB + 0.2 M LiNO₃ in PC:DME (1:3 v/v). Polymer membrane thickness: 260 µm.

### Polymerization Method

- **Polymerization Type**: In-situ free radical copolymerization
- **Polymerization Conditions**: 70°C for 6 h under argon atmosphere; AIBN thermal initiator generates free radicals to induce VC and MBA copolymerization; after precursor injection into assembled battery, gelation occurs under same temperature/time conditions, followed by 12 h standing for uniform electrolyte distribution

### Key Innovation Points

1. **Sustainable fluorine-free SEI design dominated by Li₂O through synergistic molecular engineering**: Cross-linked copolymer combining highly polar cyclic VC (main ion-conducting segment with high Li⁺ binding energy) and weakly polar MBA (crosslinker enhancing mechanical properties and serving as anionic anchoring site via hydrogen bonding) creates weak Li⁺ solvation structure. DFT calculations reveal polymerized PVM-GPE has lower LUMO energy than VC/MBA, preferentially reducing on Li metal to form N- and O-rich SEI, while VC exhibits lowest HOMO energy enhancing oxidative stability. Quantitative XPS O-elemental analysis shows Li₂O percentage content is 76% (before etching) and 28% (after etching) in PVM-GPE versus lower values in PDE, with overall O content increasing from 66% to 74% with etching depth, confirming Li₂O as primary SEI component.

2. **Anion-dominated solvation structure enabling high Li⁺ transport**: MD simulations reveal PVM-GPE forms anion-rich solvation structure with BOB⁻ dominating solvation shell. RDF analysis shows sharp peaks at 2.07, 2.04, 2.03, 2.14 Å for BOB⁻, PC, PVM coordination with Li⁺. Coordination number of Li⁺ with PVM reaches 1.68 while solvent molecule CN decreases from 3.3 (PDE) to lower values, indicating PVM facilitates solvent expulsion promoting anion-rich structure. Raman spectroscopy shows AGGs proportion decreases from 53.8% (PDE) to 35.9% (PVM-GPE) while CIPs increase to 50.4%, with solvation energy of -10.12 eV facilitating desolvation and Li⁺ hopping conduction. This results in high Li⁺ transference number (0.77), ionic conductivity (1.4×10⁻³ S cm⁻¹ at 25°C), low activation energy (10.89 kJ mol⁻¹ vs 12.13 kJ mol⁻¹ for PDE).

3. **Preferential Li(200) crystal plane growth regulation through Li₂O-rich SEI**: Ex situ XRD monitoring during Li deposition reveals PDE electrolyte predominantly exhibits Li(110) crystal plane with increasing intensity over time, while PVM-GPE shows preferential transition from (110) to (200) crystal plane with gradually increasing (200) intensity and decreasing (110) intensity. DFT surface energy calculations demonstrate PVM-GPE modulates energy relationship to PVM-GPE(200) < PVM-GPE(110), making (200) plane possess lowest surface energy and preferential orientation. This (200) plane exposure suppresses Li metal reactivity towards electrolyte, shields adverse side reactions, and enables uniform spherical Li deposition with dense flat morphology confirmed by SEM, contrasting with irregular cracked deposition in PDE.

### Special Performance

- **Wide Electrochemical Window and High-Voltage Stability**: PVM-GPE exhibits oxidation onset at 4.74 V (LSV), demonstrating high oxidation stability reliable for high-voltage applications. Leakage current test at constant 4.5 V shows PVM-GPE exhibits smaller leakage current indicating satisfactory stability. Successfully operates with NCM811 at 4.5 V cutoff voltage and NCM90 high-nickel cathode, confirming compatibility with high-voltage high-nickel positive electrodes.

- **Safety and Abuse Tolerance**: Pouch batteries pass abuse testing including bending, folding, and cutting conditions while illuminating LED, demonstrating great safety utility. Thermal stability shows 10% weight loss at ~167°C (TGA) and low glass transition temperature of -34°C (DSC) indicating rapid segmental dynamics.

- **Sustainable Fluorine-Free Design**: Completely eliminates fluorine-containing components while maintaining competitive performance with mainstream fluorinated polymer electrolytes. XPS F 1s spectrum shows ~5% F content in CEI primarily from PVDF binder rather than electrolyte decomposition, confirming fluorine-free nature. Primary CEI elements are C and O forming uniform robust interface.

- **SEI Structural Characteristics**: TEM reveals uniform SEI morphology with lattice fringes of Li₂O(111) at 0.267 nm and Li₂O(200) at 0.231 nm spacing. TOF-SIMS 3D rendering shows homogeneous distribution of LiO⁻, C₂HO⁻ elements. SEI features gradient organic-inorganic structure with Li₂O-dominated inner layer in contact with Li metal, effectively regulating uniform Li deposition and preventing parasitic reactions.

## Mechanism Analysis and Characterization

**DFT Calculations (Binding Energy and Molecular Orbitals)**: Carbonyl oxygen on PVM-GPE exhibits stronger binding energy to Li⁺ than MBA, indicating preferential Li⁺ coordination to this site. Weak binding of Li⁺ with MBA attributed to electronegativity of nitrogen reducing electron cloud density in carbonyl oxygen. LUMO energy of polymerized PVM-GPE is lower than VC and MBA, enabling preferential reduction on Li metal forming N- and O-rich SEI. VC exhibits lowest HOMO energy enhancing oxidative stability. Binding energy of PVM-Li complex (-3.71 eV) is lower than BOB-Li but higher than PC (-2.55 eV) and DME (-1.98 eV), with C-H bonds on PVM forming ion-dipole interactions with BOB⁻ restricting anion mobility and increasing tLi⁺.

**DFT Surface Adsorption Calculations (Crystal Plane Preference)**: PVM-GPE exhibits maximum adsorption energy on Li(200) crystal surface, more likely to adsorb and react during cycling. Adsorption energy calculations on Li(200) and Li(110) planes reveal overall energy state is lower for (200) plane compared to (110) plane when PVM-GPE interacts with Li surface. This difference stems from variations in atomic arrangement and bonding characteristics across crystallographic planes. Li(200) plane atomic arrangement favors formation of stable chemical bonds with Li₂O components, reducing overall system energy and resulting in more stable SEI.

**MD Simulations (Solvation Structure, 400 ps equilibrium + 400 ps data collection)**: Snapshots and local zoom comparisons demonstrate PVM-GPE successfully participates in Li⁺ solvated sheath structure formation. RDF analysis shows distinct sharp peaks at 2.07, 2.04, 2.03, 2.14 Å for BOB⁻, PC, PVM coordination with Li⁺, with BOB⁻ dominating solvation shell. CN of Li⁺ with PVM reaches 1.68 while solvent molecule CN decreases from 3.3 (PDE), indicating PVM facilitates solvent expulsion. MSD analysis shows slightly reduced diffusion coefficient for PVM-GPE, suggesting modified coordination structure contributes to Li⁺ microdynamics. Solvation energy of -10.12 eV indicates relatively low energy facilitating desolvation and hopping conduction.

**Raman Spectroscopy (Solvation Structure Validation)**: PDE system shows solvation structure predominantly characterized by AGGs (53.8% total), consistent with MD simulations. In PVM system, AGGs proportion decreases to 35.9% as polymer matrix incorporation into solvation structure occupies steric space, weakening BOB⁻-Li⁺ coordination. Original AGGs structures transform into CIPs with direct Li⁺-BOB⁻ binding (50.4%), reducing Li⁺ confinement by PVM and promoting Li⁺ migration.

**XPS Depth Profiling (SEI Composition)**: O 1s spectra show Li₂O intensity (~528 eV) in PVM-GPE is higher than PDE, with peak intensity decreasing as sputtering depth increases, confirming Li₂O as primary SEI component. Quantitative O-elemental analysis shows Li₂O percentage content is 76% (before etching) and 28% (after etching) in PVM-GPE versus lower values in PDE. Overall elemental analysis shows O content increased from 66% to 74% with etching depth. C 1s shows major C-C (~284.8 eV), C-O (~286.5 eV), O=C-O (~288 eV), C=O (~290 eV), and RCO₃ (~291 eV) from polymer decomposition. N 1s shows higher Li₃N (~398 eV) intensity in PVM-GPE indicating nitrification group participation in SEI formation. B 1s shows B-O (191.5 eV), LixNOy, and N-O compounds from reductive decomposition of BOB⁻ and NO₃⁻.

**TOF-SIMS 3D Analysis (SEI Homogeneity)**: 3D renders of LiO⁻, C₂HO⁻ fragments show homogeneous elemental distribution at SEI interface. Depth profiling confirms structure and composition consistent with XPS results. Bi⁺ ion beam at 30 keV for high-sensitivity surface excitation; Cs⁺ sputtering at 3 keV/100 nA for depth profiling.

**TEM and HRTEM (SEI Microstructure, 5 kV accelerating voltage)**: PVM-GPE exhibits uniform SEI morphology facilitating uniform Li deposition at high areal capacities. Magnified images identify lattice fringes of Li₂O(111) with 0.267 nm spacing and Li₂O(200) with 0.231 nm spacing. SADE patterns from regions A and B corroborate Li₂O presence. EDS elemental mapping reveals significantly higher O content in PVM-GPE-derived SEI. NCM811 cathode shows uniform CEI ~7 nm thick versus distinct inhomogeneity in PDE, with SADE analysis demonstrating PVM-GPE effectively maintains long-term structural stability.

**Ex situ XRD (Crystal Plane Evolution)**: After 1 h and 2 h Li deposition, PDE predominantly exhibits Li(110) crystal plane with increasing peak intensity over time. PVM-GPE shows preferential transition from (110) to (200) crystal plane with gradually increasing (200) intensity and decreasing (110) intensity, confirming (200) as predominantly exposed surface.

**In situ XRD (NCM811 Structural Stability)**: (003) characteristic diffraction peak shows offset of 0.43°, indicating small anisotropic volume change. Small offset confirms NCM811 with PVM-GPE has less volume change during cycling, effectively inhibiting structural collapse due to volume expansion. Evolutionary trends of (003), (101), (102), (104) peaks consistent with long-cycle stability.

**SEM Morphology Analysis (Li Deposition)**: PVM-GPE deposited on Cu foil shows spherical structure with dense flat morphology, while PDE shows irregular deposition with cracks forming loose pattern. Li||Li symmetric cell at 1 mA cm⁻² shows uniform flat Li metal surface with no obvious dendrite growth for PVM-GPE, confirmed by cross-sectional SEM. PDE exhibits rough surface with unstable interface.

**EIS Evolution (Interfacial Stability)**: Li||Li cells with PVM-GPE show gradually decreasing interfacial impedance during cycling (tested every 10 cycles at 1 mA cm⁻²), corresponding to lower overpotential attributed to electrolyte stability against Li metal. PDE shows impedance first increasing then decreasing, illustrating inhomogeneous Li⁺ deposition.

## Conclusions and Significance

This work presents sustainable fluorine-free gel polymer electrolyte (PVM-GPE) generating Li₂O-rich O/N-containing hybrid SEI with improved interfacial stability and Li⁺ transport kinetics, providing modern design paradigm for next-generation solid-state batteries. Key achievements include: (1) Molecular engineering through cross-linked copolymer combining highly polar cyclic VC (main ion-conducting segment) and weakly polar MBA (crosslinker providing hydrogen bonding and anionic anchoring) creates weak Li⁺ solvation structure, with DFT showing polymerized PVM-GPE has lower LUMO preferentially reducing to form N- and O-rich SEI while VC's lowest HOMO enhances oxidative stability; (2) Anion-dominated solvation structure confirmed by MD simulations (BOB⁻ dominates shell, Li⁺-PVM CN=1.68) and Raman spectroscopy (AGGs decrease from 53.8% to 35.9%, CIPs increase to 50.4%) enables high Li⁺ transference number (0.77), ionic conductivity (1.4×10⁻³ S cm⁻¹), low activation energy (10.89 kJ mol⁻¹), and exchange current density (0.41 mA cm⁻²); (3) Quantitative XPS analysis proves Li₂O-dominated SEI (76% before etching, 28% after etching) with TEM revealing uniform morphology and Li₂O(111)/Li₂O(200) lattice fringes, while ex situ XRD demonstrates preferential Li(200) crystal plane growth through DFT-validated surface energy modulation enabling uniform spherical deposition; (4) Li||Li symmetric cells achieve >1500 h stable cycling at 1 mA cm⁻² (0.26 V polarization), >100 h at 3 mA cm⁻²; Li||NCM811 full cells deliver 84.7% capacity retention over 800 cycles at 3 C with excellent rate capability (196.4 mAh g⁻¹ at 0.1 C to 117.1 mAh g⁻¹ at 5 C, >99% recovery), successful operation at 4.5 V high-voltage and compatibility with NCM90 high-nickel cathode; (5) Pouch cells achieve 208 mAh g⁻¹ at 0.2 C with >150 cycles stability, 1 Ah pouch battery (0.996 Ah capacity) passes abuse testing (bending, folding, cutting) demonstrating safety, confirming practical application potential. This fluorine-free SEI strategy completely eliminates environmental/cost concerns of fluorinated reagents while maintaining competitive high-rate performance versus mainstream fluorinated polymer electrolytes, laying solid foundation for sustainable quasi-solid-state battery design and advancing practical high-energy-density Li metal batteries for electric aviation, deep space exploration, and underwater vehicle applications.
