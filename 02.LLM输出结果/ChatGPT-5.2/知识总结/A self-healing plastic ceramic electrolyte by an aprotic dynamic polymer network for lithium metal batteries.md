## Research Background and Motivation

Oxide ceramic electrolytes (OCEs) are attractive for solid-state lithium metal batteries due to high modulus and thermal stability, but their practical application is hindered by low grain-boundary ionic conductivity, Li dendrite penetration, interfacial instability with Li metal, mechanical brittleness, and complicated high-pressure sintering processes. Defects such as cracks and voids act as current hotspots that accelerate dendrite growth and mechanical failure, leading to low critical current density (<1 mA cm⁻²) and short cycle life. Existing strategies address individual problems but fail to simultaneously solve conductivity, interface, mechanics, and manufacturability. Therefore, a fundamentally new electrolyte design that can autonomously repair internal defects while maintaining high ionic conductivity is required.

## Research Methods and Innovation Points

### Composition of Gel Electrolyte (PCE)

| Component                     | Specific Material                                | Content/Ratio  |
| ----------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------ | -------------- |
| **Polymer Matrix/Monomer**    | Ethyl acrylate (EA) + lithium methacrylate MTFSI | In SH-SPE      |
| **Lithium Salt**              | LiTFSI                                           | 0.6 g (SH-SPE) |
| **Solvent/Plasticizer**       | Succinonitrile (SN)                              | 1 g            |
| **Additive**                  | Fluoroethylene carbonate (FEC)                   | 5 wt%          |
| **Initiator**                 | PPO (photoinitiator)                             | 0.1 wt%        |
| **Ceramic Filler**            | Li1.5Al0.5Ti1.5(PO4)3 (LATP)                     | 70 wt%         |
| **Polymerization Conditions** | 365 nm UV, 10 min, room temperature              |                |

Final electrolyte: **PCE = 70 wt% LATP + 30 wt% SH-SPE**

---

### Polymerization Method

* **Polymerization Type**: In-situ free-radical photopolymerization
* **Polymerization Conditions**: Solvent-free precursor, UV irradiation (365 nm, 10 min, 22 °C)

---

### Key Innovation Points

1. Aprotic dynamic polymer network based on reversible –CH₃···CF₃ non-covalent bonding enabling intrinsic self-healing.
2. Dual-phase self-healing mechanism: polymer infiltration followed by ceramic particle migration to repair defects.
3. Plastic ceramic electrolyte concept combining ceramic modulus with polymer adaptability, overcoming brittleness of conventional OCEs.

## Mechanism Analysis and Characterization

| Method               | Key Finding                                                 |
| -------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------- |
| Operando XRF         | Direct visualization of two-step self-healing process       |
| Cryo-TEM             | Dense, dendrite-free Li deposition with uniform SEI         |
| 2D EXSY NMR          | Fast Li⁺ exchange between polymer and LATP phases           |
| ⁶Li isotope exchange | Uniform Li⁺ transport through both phases                   |
| XAS/XPS              | Stable SEI (LiF, Li₂O, Li₂Sx) and suppressed LATP reduction |

Mechanism: Dynamic polymer chains infiltrate defects to eliminate current hotspots, followed by LATP migration to restore mechanical integrity.

---

## Conclusions and Significance

This study reports a self-healing plastic ceramic electrolyte that simultaneously addresses conductivity, mechanical stability, interfacial compatibility, and manufacturability challenges of oxide solid electrolytes. By integrating a dynamically crosslinked aprotic polymer with LATP ceramic, grain-boundary resistance is reduced by 33× and dendrite-induced failure is suppressed for >4000 h. Full cells achieve >3600 cycles under near-zero pressure. This work establishes self-healing hybrid solid electrolytes as a new paradigm for practical solid-state lithium metal batteries.
