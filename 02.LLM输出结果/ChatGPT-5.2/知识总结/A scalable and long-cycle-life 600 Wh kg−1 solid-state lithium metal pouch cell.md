## Research Background and Motivation

Emerging applications such as eVTOLs, UAVs, and electric ships require batteries with ≥600 Wh kg⁻¹ specific energy, far beyond current Li-ion batteries (~360 Wh kg⁻¹). Carbonate electrolytes are stable at high voltage but react severely with Li metal, causing low reversibility and short cycle life under lean electrolyte conditions. Practical deployment of high-energy LMBs is limited by interface instability, safety, and manufacturability.

## Mechanism Analysis

The in-situ polymer backbone anchors carbonate solvents, reducing free solvent activity and suppressing parasitic reactions. BGPL homogenizes Li⁺ flux and stabilizes SEI, enabling dense, dendrite-free Li plating. The synergy minimizes gas evolution, electrode expansion, and interfacial impedance. 

---

## Conclusions and Significance

This work demonstrates a scalable strategy integrating gel-solid-state electrolyte and BGPL-modified Li metal to realize Ah-class pouch cells with 600 Wh kg⁻¹ specific energy, long cycle life, and high safety, representing a milestone toward practical high-energy lithium metal batteries.
