## Research Background and Motivation

Conventional gel polymer electrolytes (GPEs) and liquid electrolytes typically rely on fluorinated lithium salts (e.g., LiPF₆, LiTFSI), which decompose to form LiF-rich SEI. Although LiF is mechanically robust, it exhibits low Li⁺ ionic conductivity, leading to high interfacial resistance and nonuniform Li deposition. Moreover, fluorinated electrolytes raise cost and environmental concerns. This work aims to design a fluorine-free GPE that can construct a lithium oxide (Li₂O)-rich SEI with higher ionic conductivity to stabilize Li metal anodes.

## Research Methods and Innovation Points

### Composition of Gel Electrolyte

| Component                        | Specific Material                        | Content/Ratio                  |
| -------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------- | ------------------------------ |
| **Polymer Matrix/Monomer**       | Poly(ethylene glycol) diacrylate (PEGDA) | In situ polymerized            |
| **Lithium Salt**                 | LiClO₄                                   | 1 M                            |
| **Solvent**                      | Dimethyl carbonate (DMC)                 | Major solvent                  |
| **Additive**                     | None (fluorine-free system)              | —                              |
| **Initiator/Crosslinking Agent** | Azobisisobutyronitrile (AIBN)            | Small amount                   |
| **Polymerization Conditions**    | 60 °C, 2 h                               | In situ thermal polymerization |

### Polymerization Method

* **Polymerization Type**: In-situ radical polymerization
* **Polymerization Conditions**: Thermal initiation at 60 °C inside assembled cells

### Key Innovation Points

1. Completely fluorine-free GPE system, avoiding LiF-dominated SEI formation.
2. Induction of Li₂O-rich SEI with high Li⁺ conductivity for uniform Li plating/stripping.
3. In-situ polymerized PEGDA network enabling intimate electrode/electrolyte interfacial contact.

## Mechanism Analysis and Characterization

XPS depth profiling reveals that the SEI formed in the fluorine-free GPE is dominated by Li₂O and Li₂CO₃ rather than LiF. TEM shows a thin, uniform, and compact SEI layer. The Li₂O-rich SEI provides higher Li⁺ conductivity and homogeneous ion flux, suppressing dendritic growth. 

## Conclusions and Significance

This study demonstrates a fluorine-free in-situ polymerized GPE that constructs a Li₂O-rich SEI with superior ionic transport properties, enabling long-life and dendrite-free Li metal batteries. The work provides a new paradigm for SEI chemistry regulation beyond LiF-dominated systems, offering a cost-effective and environmentally benign strategy for next-generation high-energy batteries.
