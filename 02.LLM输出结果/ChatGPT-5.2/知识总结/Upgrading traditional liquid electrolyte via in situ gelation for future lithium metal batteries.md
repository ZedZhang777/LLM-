## Research Background and Motivation

Lithium metal batteries (LMBs) offer ultra-high theoretical energy density but suffer from severe lithium dendrite growth, interfacial instability, and safety issues when using conventional liquid electrolytes. Existing gel or solid polymer electrolytes improve lithium compatibility but usually require additional monomers, initiators, or harsh conditions, limiting scalability and compatibility with commercial electrolytes. This work aims to develop a facile, impurity-free, and industry-compatible strategy to convert conventional liquid electrolytes into quasi-solid gel polymer electrolytes (GPEs) under ambient conditions.

## Research Methods and Innovation Points

### Composition of Gel Electrolyte

| Component                          | Specific Material                         | Content / Ratio     |
| ---------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------- | ------------------- |
| **Polymer Matrix / Monomer**       | 1,3-Dioxolane (DOL)                       | In situ polymerized |
| **Lithium Salt**                   | LiPF₆ (2 M); LiTFSI (1 M)                 | As stated           |
| **Solvent**                        | 1,2-Dimethoxyethane (DME)                 | DOL:DME = 1:1 (v/v) |
| **Additive**                       | None                                      | —                   |
| **Initiator / Crosslinking Agent** | PF₅ (from LiPF₆ decomposition)            | In situ generated   |
| **Polymerization Conditions**      | Ambient temperature, no external stimulus | —                   |

### Polymerization Method

* **Polymerization Type**: In situ cationic ring-opening polymerization
* **Polymerization Conditions**: Room temperature; PF₅ generated from partial LiPF₆ decomposition initiates DOL polymerization

### Key Innovation Points

1. Discovery and utilization of LiPF₆-induced cationic ring-opening polymerization of DOL inside batteries.
2. Direct conversion of a fully commercial liquid electrolyte into a quasi-solid GPE without introducing foreign monomers or initiators.
3. Broad compatibility with multiple cathode chemistries (S, LiFePO₄, NCM622) and extended electrochemical stability up to high voltages.

### Special Performance

* **Wide-Temperature / High-Voltage Stability**: Electrochemical stability extended to ~4.6 V, enabling compatibility with NCM622.
* **Safety and Stability**: Reduced volatility, suppressed lithium dendrites, stable SEI formation.
* **Other Characteristics**: Effective polysulfide confinement, low self-discharge, mechanical flexibility suitable for soft-pack batteries.

## Mechanism Analysis and Characterization

The gelation mechanism is attributed to LiPF₆ partial decomposition releasing PF₅, which acts as a Lewis acid to initiate cationic ring-opening polymerization of DOL, forming high-molecular-weight polydioxolane (PDXL). The polymer framework immobilizes the electrolyte while DME serves as a plasticizer. Mechanisms were validated by GC–MS (PF₅ detection), NMR (¹H/¹³C), FTIR, XPS, GPC (Mn ≈ 52,000), ionic conductivity tracking, and electrochemical impedance spectroscopy.

## Conclusions and Significance

This work presents a highly practical and scalable strategy to upgrade conventional ether-based liquid electrolytes into quasi-solid GPEs via in situ polymerization driven by LiPF₆. The resulting GPE significantly enhances lithium metal stability, suppresses dendrites, mitigates polysulfide shuttling, and broadens the electrochemical window to high-voltage cathodes. Owing to its use of fully commercial materials and ambient processing conditions, this approach offers strong potential for industrial adoption and represents a new paradigm for electrolyte design in next-generation lithium metal batteries.
