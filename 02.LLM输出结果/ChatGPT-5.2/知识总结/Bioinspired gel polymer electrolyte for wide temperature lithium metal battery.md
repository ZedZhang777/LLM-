## Research Background and Motivation

Conventional liquid electrolytes suffer from leakage, flammability, and poor low-temperature performance, while solid electrolytes exhibit high interfacial resistance and poor flexibility. Existing gel polymer electrolytes (GPEs) often face a trade-off between ionic conductivity and mechanical robustness, especially under wide-temperature operation. Therefore, developing a safe, flexible, and high-conductivity electrolyte operable across a wide temperature range is essential for practical lithium metal batteries.

### Polymerization Method

* **Polymerization Type**: In-situ radical polymerization
* **Polymerization Conditions**: 60 °C thermal initiation using AIBN

### Key Innovation Points

1. Bioinspired network structure mimicking biological tissues to achieve high flexibility and toughness.
2. Introduction of flame-retardant TFEP to improve safety and wide-temperature stability.
3. In-situ polymerization to ensure intimate electrode–electrolyte interfacial contact.

### Special Performance

* **Wide-Temperature Performance**: Stable operation from −40 °C to 60 °C with high ionic conductivity retention.
* **Safety**: Excellent flame retardancy and thermal stability due to TFEP incorporation.
* **Other Characteristic Performance**: Suppression of lithium dendrite growth enabled by elastic polymer network.

## Mechanism Analysis and Characterization

XPS and FTIR analyses reveal strong coordination between Li⁺ and ether oxygen in PEG chains, facilitating fast ion transport. SEM confirms uniform lithium deposition morphology. Molecular dynamics simulations demonstrate reduced Li⁺ solvation energy and enhanced interfacial compatibility.

## Conclusions and Significance

This work reports a bioinspired in-situ polymerized gel electrolyte that simultaneously achieves high ionic conductivity, mechanical robustness, and safety across a wide temperature range. The strategy effectively addresses the long-standing trade-off between flexibility and electrochemical performance, providing a promising electrolyte design paradigm for next-generation high-energy lithium metal batteries.
