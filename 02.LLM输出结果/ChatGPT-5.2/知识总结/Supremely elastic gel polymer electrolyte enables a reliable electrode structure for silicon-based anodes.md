## Research Background and Motivation

Silicon-based anodes suffer from extreme volume expansion and contraction during lithiation/delithiation, leading to particle displacement, electrode cracking, loss of electrical contact, unstable SEI, and rapid capacity fading. Existing strategies based on nanostructuring and advanced binders mainly repair damage after it occurs and remain ineffective at the electrode level, especially under high mass loading. An intrinsic strategy to suppress particle displacement and electrode cracking is urgently needed.

## Research Methods and Innovation Points

### Composition of Gel Electrolyte

| Component                     | Specific Material                                                                                                          | Content/Ratio                                           |
| ----------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Polymer Matrix**            | Poly(poly(tetramethylene ether) glycol-co-4,4’-methylene diphenyl diisocyanate)-ethylene diamine) (PTMG–MDI–EDA copolymer) | Soft PTMG : Hard MDI–EDA = 2 : 1 (optimal, Copolymer 1) |
| **Lithium Salt**              | LiPF₆                                                                                                                      | 1 M                                                     |
| **Solvent**                   | EC/DEC                                                                                                                     | 1:1 (v/v)                                               |
| **Additive**                  | FEC                                                                                                                        | 10 wt% (battery electrolyte)                            |
| **Initiator/Crosslinker**     | None (step-growth polymerization)                                                                                          | —                                                       |
| **Polymerization Conditions** | MDI + PTMG at 80 °C (4 h), then EDA at 70 °C (4 h)                                                                         | —                                                       |

### Polymerization Method

* **Polymerization Type**: Step-growth polyurethane–urea copolymerization
* **Polymerization Conditions**: Solution polymerization in dimethylacetamide, 70–80 °C

### Key Innovation Points

1. Introduction of a supremely elastic GPE acting as an intra-electrode cushion to intrinsically suppress SiO particle displacement and electrode cracking.
2. Unique soft–hard domain copolymer design combining PTMG (elasticity, swelling) and MDI–EDA (mechanical constraint).
3. Uniform self-infusion of GPE inside electrodes to maintain ionic conductivity while reinforcing mechanical integrity.

### Special Performance

* **Structural Stability**: Electrode thickness expansion reduced from 94% (bare) to 53% (with GPE) during first lithiation.
* **Interfacial Stability**: GPE participates in SEI formation, suppressing inorganic salt accumulation.
* **High Coulombic Efficiency**: ~99.9% over 300 cycles in full cells.

## Mechanism Analysis and Characterization

In situ electrode thickness measurements and SEM reveal that the elastic GPE suppresses particle displacement and electrode cracking. XPS shows polymer-derived species in SEI and reduced inorganic salt content, indicating moderated electrolyte decomposition. Mechanical tests confirm ultra-high elasticity enabling reversible deformation during cycling.

## Conclusions and Significance

A supremely elastic gel polymer electrolyte with a soft–hard domain copolymer architecture intrinsically stabilizes silicon-based anodes by acting as an internal mechanical buffer. This strategy effectively suppresses electrode-level structural failure while maintaining high ionic conductivity, enabling long-life, high-areal-capacity SiO||NCM cells. The work provides a new paradigm for mechanically adaptive electrolytes in high-energy lithium-ion batteries.
