## Research Background and Motivation

Lithium metal batteries (LMBs) suffer from uncontrolled lithium dendrite growth and unstable solid electrolyte interphase (SEI), which lead to poor cycling stability and safety risks, especially under practical low N/P ratio conditions. Conventional liquid electrolytes form fragile and heterogeneous SEI layers, unable to suppress dendrites effectively. Therefore, constructing a robust, uniform, and multifunctional SEI via electrolyte engineering is essential for enabling high-energy-density LMBs.

* **Polymerization Conditions**: Thermal initiation at 60 °C for 2 h inside the assembled cell

### Key Innovation Points

1. In-situ polymerization constructs a conformal and robust multifunctional SEI directly on lithium metal.
2. The polymer-rich SEI simultaneously regulates Li⁺ flux and enhances mechanical stability.
3. Enables stable cycling under practical low N/P ratio conditions.

## Mechanism Analysis and Characterization

XPS and TOF-SIMS reveal a polymer-rich, inorganic–organic hybrid SEI containing LiF, Li₃N, and polymeric species. SEM and in situ optical microscopy confirm uniform lithium deposition without dendrite formation. Finite element simulations demonstrate homogenized Li⁺ flux and reduced interfacial stress due to the elastic polymer network.

## Conclusions and Significance

This work demonstrates that in-situ polymerization can construct a multifunctional SEI that integrates ionic regulation and mechanical reinforcement. The strategy effectively suppresses dendrites and enables long-term stable cycling of LMBs under practical low N/P ratios, providing a scalable pathway toward high-energy-density and safe lithium metal batteries.
