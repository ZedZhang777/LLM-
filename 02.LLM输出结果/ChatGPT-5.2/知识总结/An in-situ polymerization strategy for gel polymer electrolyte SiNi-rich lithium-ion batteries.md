## Research Background and Motivation

High-nickel cathodes combined with Si-based anodes suffer from severe interfacial instability, electrolyte decomposition, and mechanical degradation during cycling. Conventional liquid electrolytes show poor interfacial compatibility and safety risks, while ex-situ prepared gel polymer electrolytes often have insufficient electrode wetting and high interfacial resistance. Therefore, a strategy enabling intimate electrode–electrolyte contact and stable interphases is required for high-energy-density Si||Ni-rich lithium-ion batteries.

## Research Methods and Innovation Points

### Composition of Gel Electrolyte

| Component                        | Specific Material                        | Content/Ratio      |
| -------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------- | ------------------ |
| **Polymer Matrix/Monomer**       | Poly(ethylene glycol) diacrylate (PEGDA) | 15 wt%             |
| **Lithium Salt**                 | LiPF₆                                    | 1.0 M              |
| **Solvent**                      | EC/DMC (1:1 v/v)                         | Balance            |
| **Additive**                     | Fluoroethylene carbonate (FEC)           | 5 wt%              |
| **Initiator/Crosslinking Agent** | Azobisisobutyronitrile (AIBN)            | 1 wt% (to monomer) |
| **Polymerization Conditions**    | 60 °C, 2 h, sealed cell                  |                    |

### Polymerization Method

* **Polymerization Type**: In-situ free-radical polymerization
* **Polymerization Conditions**: Thermal initiation at 60 °C for 2 h after cell assembly

### Key Innovation Points

1. In-situ polymerization inside the assembled battery ensures conformal electrolyte/electrode contact.
2. Formation of stable SEI on Si anode and CEI on Ni-rich cathode via polymer-derived interphases.
3. Simultaneous stabilization of high-capacity Si anode and high-voltage Ni-rich cathode in full cells.

## Mechanism Analysis and Characterization

XPS reveals LiF- and polymer-rich SEI on Si anode and stable CEI on Ni-rich cathode. SEM and TEM show uniform interphase layers suppressing particle cracking. EIS confirms reduced interfacial resistance after in-situ polymerization. FTIR verifies complete polymerization of acrylate groups inside the cell.

## Conclusions and Significance

The in-situ polymerized gel electrolyte significantly enhances interfacial stability and cycling durability of Si||Ni-rich full cells. This work demonstrates a practical strategy to integrate high-energy-density electrodes with safe quasi-solid electrolytes, offering strong potential for next-generation lithium-ion batteries.
