## Research Background and Motivation

Solid-state lithium–sulfur (Li–S) batteries promise high energy density and improved safety, but suffer from severe interfacial instability between solid electrolytes and both lithium metal anodes and sulfur cathodes. Rigid polymer electrolytes cannot accommodate large volume changes of sulfur cathodes and lithium plating/stripping, leading to interfacial cracks, high resistance, and rapid capacity decay. Existing solid polymer electrolytes also show insufficient room-temperature ionic conductivity. Therefore, a polymer electrolyte capable of autonomously repairing interfacial damage while maintaining high ionic conductivity is urgently needed.

### Key Innovation Points

1. Introduction of dynamic hydrogen-bonding networks enabling autonomous interfacial self-healing.
2. Construction of a soft, viscoelastic polymer electrolyte that maintains intimate contact with both electrodes during cycling.
3. Simultaneous stabilization of Li metal anode and sulfur cathode interfaces in solid-state Li–S batteries.

## Mechanism Analysis and Characterization

FTIR and solid-state NMR reveal reversible hydrogen-bond interactions within the polymer network, responsible for dynamic self-healing behavior. SEM cross-sectional imaging demonstrates that interfacial cracks disappear after resting, confirming autonomous interface repair. Electrochemical impedance spectroscopy shows reduced interfacial resistance after healing. Symmetric cell tests verify uniform Li plating/stripping enabled by the adaptive interface.

## Conclusions and Significance

This work demonstrates a self-healing polymer electrolyte that dynamically repairs electrode–electrolyte interfaces, enabling long-life solid-state Li–S batteries. By combining high ionic conductivity with interfacial adaptability, the study provides a new design paradigm for durable solid-state batteries and significantly advances the practical development of high-energy-density Li–S systems.
