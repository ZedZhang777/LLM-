## Research Background and Motivation

The commercialization of high-energy Lithium Metal Batteries (LMBs) is impeded by uncontrolled Li dendrite growth and pulverization in conventional liquid electrolytes (LEs). While gel/solid polymer electrolytes (GPEs/SPEs) offer improved stability, most in-situ polymerization strategies require extra monomers/initiators and harsh conditions, complicating fabrication and introducing impurities. This research is necessary to develop a facile, impurity-free strategy to upgrade commercial liquid electrolytes into high-performance GPEs using only conventional battery materials under mild conditions, enabling practical LMBs.

## Research Methods and Innovation Points

### Composition of Gel Electrolyte

| Component | Specific Material | Content/Ratio |
|---------|---------|----------|
| **Polymer Matrix/Monomer** | 1,3-Dioxolane (DOL) | Solvent/Monomer (Part of DOL/DME (1:1 v/v) mixture) |
| **Lithium Salt** | LiPF₆, LiTFSI | LiPF₆: 2 M (added for gelation); LiTFSI: 1 M (primary salt in precursor LE) |
| **Solvent** | DOL, 1,2-Dimethoxyethane (DME) | DOL/DME = 1:1 (v/v) in precursor LE |
| **Additive** | None | -- |
| **Initiator/Crosslinking Agent** | LiPF₆ (source of PF₅ Lewis acid) | 2 M |
| **Polymerization Conditions** | In-situ, ambient temperature, spontaneous gelation. | Room temperature, standing for ~10 hours. |

### Polymerization Method

- **Polymerization Type**: Cationic Ring-Opening Polymerization of DOL.
- **Polymerization Conditions**: Ambient temperature, initiated by PF₅ generated from thermal decomposition of dissolved LiPF₆. Trace H₂O acts as chain transfer agent. Process is spontaneous.

### Key Innovation Points

1.  **Novel and Simple Gelation Mechanism:** First report of using commercial LiPF₆ to induce the cationic ring-opening polymerization of the common ether solvent DOL, transforming a liquid electrolyte into a quasi-solid GPE spontaneously at room temperature.
2.  **All-Commercial, Additive-Free System:** The strategy uses only conventional battery electrolyte materials (DOL/DME, LiTFSI, LiPF₆) without introducing any foreign monomers, initiators, or additives, ensuring compatibility with existing battery manufacturing.
3.  **Universal Cathode Compatibility & Voltage Window Expansion:** The derived GPE demonstrates superior performance and stability with diverse cathodes (S, LiFePO₄, NCM622) and significantly widens the electrochemical stability window of the DOL/DME system to ~4.6 V, enabling its use with high-voltage layered oxide cathodes.

### Special Performance

- **Wide-Temperature Performance**: Not explicitly discussed for extreme temperatures, but GPE shows good reversibility to temperature variation during formation (Fig. S12).
- **Safety**: Implied improved safety due to quasi-solid state, reduced volatility, and suppressed dendrite growth. Flame retardancy not tested.
- **Other Characteristic Performance**: **Suppresses Polysulfide Shuttle** in Li-S batteries; **Expands Voltage Window** for ether electrolytes; Enables **Flexible Battery** operation due to gel mechanics.

## Mechanism Analysis and Characterization

Key mechanistic insights were obtained through:
*   **¹H/¹³C NMR & FTIR**: Confirmed the structure of the polymer product as linear polydioxolane (PDXL) with repeating unit -CH₂-O-CH₂-CH₂-O-.
*   **GC-MS & TGA**: Detected PF₅ gas from LiPF₆ decomposition, identifying it as the Lewis acid initiator for the cationic polymerization.
*   **GPC & Conductivity Monitoring**: Tracked the gelation process, showing increasing polymer molecular weight (Mn ~52,000) and stabilizing ionic conductivity.
*   **LSV**: Demonstrated the widened electrochemical stability window of GPE (~4.6 V) vs. LE (<4.2 V).
*   **SEM & XPS**: Showed smooth, dendrite-free Li morphology with GPE vs. pulverized, dendritic Li with LE. In Li-S cells, XPS confirmed GPE prevents sulfide deposition on Li anode.
*   **Permeation Test & Optical Photos**: Visually demonstrated the GPE's effective blockade of polysulfide diffusion.

## Conclusions and Significance

This work presents a remarkably simple and efficient strategy to upgrade conventional DOL/DME liquid electrolyte into a high-performance quasi-solid GPE via LiPF₆-induced in situ cationic ring-opening polymerization at room temperature. The resulting GPE effectively suppresses Li dendrite growth, enables stable Li metal cycling, and demonstrates universal compatibility with diverse cathodes (S, LiFePO₄, NCM622). Critically, it breaks the voltage limit of ether-based electrolytes, allowing stable cycling of NCM622 up to 4.3V. Its significance lies in providing a scalable, cost-effective, and manufacturing-friendly route to produce high-performance GPEs using entirely commercial materials, presenting a highly promising pathway for the practical realization of safe and high-energy lithium metal batteries.
