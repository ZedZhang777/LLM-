## Research Background and Motivation
This research addresses the critical challenges of conventional liquid electrolytes (flammability) and existing solid-state polymer electrolytes (SPEs), which suffer from low ionic conductivity, low Li⁺ transference number (tLi⁺), narrow voltage window, poor compatibility with Li-metal anodes (LMAs), and high flammability. While polycarbonate-based SPEs offer some advantages, they struggle with Li salt dissociation and lack flame retardancy. This work is necessary to develop a high-performance, non-flammable SPE via a rational molecular design strategy that simultaneously achieves high ionic conductivity, high tLi⁺, wide electrochemical stability, LMA compatibility, and superior safety for high-voltage, high-specific-energy lithium metal batteries.

### Polymerization Method
- **Polymerization Type**: In-situ Free Radical Polymerization
- **Polymerization Conditions**: Temperature: 70°C, Time: 6 h, Initiation Method: Thermal initiation by AIBN.

### Key Innovation Points
1. **Puzzle-like Molecular Assembly Strategy:** A novel design approach splicing three distinct functional molecular fragments (VEC for Li⁺ conduction, HFBMA for high-voltage stability/fluorine-rich SEI, TAP for flame retardancy/anion affinity) into a single SPE (IWSNW-SPE) via facile in-situ polymerization.
2. **Simultaneous Multi-property Enhancement:** The synergistic effect of the assembled fragments concurrently delivers high ionic conductivity (4.32×10⁻⁴ S cm⁻¹), high Li⁺ transference number (0.70), wide electrochemical window (5.15 V), excellent LMA compatibility, and intrinsic non-flammability.
3. **Practical High-Energy-Density Demonstration:** Successful assembly and testing of a 2.6-Ah Li||NCM811 pouch cell achieving a high specific energy of 349 Wh kg⁻¹, validated with rigorous safety tests (nail penetration, ARC), showcasing significant potential for practical application.

### Special Performance
- **Wide-Temperature Performance**: Stable operation at high temperature (100°C) demonstrated with Li||LFP cell. Low-temperature performance not explicitly provided.
- **Safety**: **Intrinsic non-flammability** (passes direct flame test). Pouch cells pass nail penetration test with minimal temperature rise and show superior thermal stability in ARC tests (higher onset and triggering temperatures for thermal runaway than liquid electrolyte).
- **Other Characteristic Performance**: Enables ultra-high positive electrode mass loading (15.8 mg cm⁻²). Achieves high specific energy (349 Wh kg⁻¹) in a 2.6-Ah Li||NCM811 pouch cell.

## Mechanism Analysis and Characterization
- **MD Simulations & Raman/ssNMR**: Revealed that the IWSNW-SPE promotes stronger LiTFSI dissociation and creates a more diverse Li⁺ coordination environment (with C=O from VEC and P=O from TAP) compared to pure PVEC. It also shows stronger interaction with TFSI⁻ anions, restricting their mobility and contributing to the high tLi⁺.
- **DFT Calculations**: Confirmed the IWSNW-SPE unit has the lowest HOMO energy (-8.04 eV), explaining its high oxidative stability (5.15 V), and a higher LUMO energy than VEC, indicating improved reductive stability.
- **XPS Depth Profiling & TOF-SIMS**: On cycled Li anodes, the IWSNW-SPE formed an SEI richer in inorganic LiF and containing LiPO₃, with a thinner organic outer layer compared to the organic-rich SEI from PVEC.
- **Cryo-TEM & EDS**: Directly visualized a uniform, dual-layered SEI on Li metal with an inorganic-rich inner layer (containing LiF crystallites) and an amorphous outer layer. Elemental mapping showed uniform distribution of F, O, and P.
- **XPS & TOF-SIMS on Cathode**: The IWSNW-SPE formed a thin (~2 nm), uniform CEI on NCM622 rich in LiF and phosphate species, effectively suppressing transition metal dissolution and structural degradation, unlike the thick, uneven CEI from PVEC.

## Conclusions and Significance
This work successfully developed a high-performance, non-flammable solid-state polymer electrolyte (IWSNW-SPE) via a novel "puzzle-like" molecular assembly strategy. By in-situ copolymerizing VEC, HFBMA, and TAP functional fragments, the SPE simultaneously achieves high ionic conductivity (4.32×10⁻⁴ S cm⁻¹), high Li⁺ transference number (0.70), a wide electrochemical window (5.15 V), excellent compatibility with Li metal (symmetrical cell >2600 h), and intrinsic flame retardancy. The designed electrolyte facilitates LiTFSI dissociation, restricts anion mobility, and fosters the formation of robust, inorganic-rich SEI/CEI layers. This enables long-cycling high-voltage Li||NCM622 cells (300 cycles) even with high mass loading (15.8 mg cm⁻²), stable operation at 100°C, and the assembly of a high-energy-density (349 Wh kg⁻¹) pouch cell with validated superior safety. This molecular design strategy provides a versatile and scalable pathway for developing customizable, high-performance, and safe SPEs for next-generation lithium metal batteries.
