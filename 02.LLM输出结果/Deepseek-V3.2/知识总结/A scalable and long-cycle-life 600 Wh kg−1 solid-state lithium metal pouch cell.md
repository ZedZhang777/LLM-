## Research Background and Motivation

This research aims to develop high-specific-energy (>600 Wh kg⁻¹), long-cycle-life lithium metal batteries (LMBs) for demanding applications like electric aviation (eVTOLs, UAVs) and maritime transport (BPSs). Current Li-ion batteries (~360 Wh kg⁻¹) fall short of the minimum energy requirements for these applications (≥400-600 Wh kg⁻¹). While LMBs promise higher energy density, achieving cell-level specific energy ≥600 Wh kg⁻¹ under lean electrolyte conditions is challenging due to parasitic reactions between Li metal and conventional carbonate electrolytes, electrolyte depletion, and instability of high-voltage, high-Ni cathodes. Existing ether-based electrolytes offer good Li compatibility but suffer from poor oxidation stability, Al corrosion, and safety issues (gassing, thermal instability). This work proposes a synergistic strategy integrating an engineered carbonate-based gel-solid-state electrolyte with a surface-modified Li anode to overcome these barriers and enable scalable production of practical, high-energy LMBs.

## Research Methods and Innovation Points

### Composition of Gel Electrolyte

| Component | Specific Material | Content/Ratio |
|---------|---------|----------|
| **Polymer Matrix/Monomer** | Pentaerythritol tetraacrylate (PETE), Diethyl allyl phosphate (DAP), 2,2,3,4,4,4-hexafluorobutyl methacrylate (HFBMA) | 1.5 wt% PETE, 1.5 wt% DAP, 1 wt% HFBMA (in precursor) |
| **Lithium Salt** | LiPF₆ | 1.2 M |
| **Solvent** | FEC/FEMC/EMC/DMC | v/v/v/v = 3:1.5:1.5:4 |
| **Additive** | Mg(TFSI)₂, LiNO₃, Hexamethyldisilazane (HMDS) | 0.02 M Mg(TFSI)₂, 65 mM LiNO₃, 0.1 wt% HMDS |
| **Initiator/Crosslinking Agent** | Benzoyl Peroxide (BPO) | 0.2 wt% |
| **Polymerization Conditions** | Heating in vacuum oven | 65°C for 4h (coin cells); 60°C for 1h (pouch cells) |

### Polymerization Method

- **Polymerization Type**: In-situ free radical polymerization.
- **Polymerization Conditions**: Thermally initiated at 60-65°C for 1-4 hours in vacuum.

### Key Innovation Points

1. **Synergistic Electrolyte & Anode Engineering**: Combines an in-situ polymerized gel-solid-state electrolyte (GMFN) with a bicontinuous gradient polymer layer (BGPL) on Li metal (BGPL@Li) to simultaneously address cathode stability, Li reversibility, and electrolyte depletion.
2. **Molecular Anchoring in Gel Electrolyte**: The designed crosslinked polymer backbone (PETE-DAP-HFBMA) effectively confines free carbonate solvent molecules via dipole-dipole interactions, suppressing parasitic reactions and enhancing oxidative stability.
3. **Multifunctional Artificial SEI (BGPL)**: A solution-processed, scalable BGPL combines lithiophilic poly(1,3,5-trioxane) (PTX) and hydrophobic PVDF-co-HFP, providing high Li⁺ conductivity, mechanical robustness, and exceptional moisture/oxygen resistance, enabling damage-free Li processing and stable long-term cycling.

### Special Performance

- **High Specific Energy**: Achieved 604.2 Wh kg⁻¹ at the pouch cell level, surpassing the 600 Wh kg⁻¹ threshold for aviation applications.
- **Safety**: The GMFN electrolyte is non-flammable. Pouch cells passed nail penetration tests without thermal runaway at 25% and 100% SOC, showing significantly improved safety over liquid electrolyte cells.
- **Environmental Stability**: BGPL@Li foil remained stable (>96% active Li preserved) after 240h in dry air (dew point -30°C), whereas bare Li corroded completely. Excellent hydrophobicity and oxygen resistance demonstrated.
- **Scalable Processing**: The BGPL coating enables damage-free, roll-to-roll processing of thin Li foil (50-60 µm), addressing a major manufacturing hurdle for LMBs.

## Mechanism Analysis and Characterization

Key mechanistic insights were obtained through multi-faceted characterization:
*   **Molecular Dynamics (MD) Simulations & Raman Spectroscopy**: Revealed that the polymer backbone in GMFN has stronger binding energy with carbonate solvents (via C=O...C-F/P=O dipole interactions) than solvent-solvent interactions, effectively confining free solvent molecules and reducing their diffusion coefficient.
*   **NMR (¹H, ⁷Li, ¹⁹F, HOESY) & WAXS**: Corroborated the strong polymer-solvent interaction and indicated an increased proportion of Li⁺-PF₆⁻ ion pairs in GMFN, favoring inorganic-rich SEI formation.
*   **XPS & TOF-SIMS**: On the **anode**, GMFN promoted a thinner, inorganic-rich (LiF) SEI. On the **cathode**, GMFN formed a thin (2.3 nm), uniform CEI rich in PO₃⁻ and Li₃Fₓ₊₁ species, compared to a thick (14.5 nm), organic-rich CEI from liquid LP334 electrolyte.
*   **GC-MS**: GMFN-based pouch cells generated negligible CO₂, CO, and O₂ gases upon cycling, indicating suppressed electrolyte oxidation at the high-voltage cathode.
*   **Electrochemical Tests**: High Li⁺ transference number (0.64), low leakage current in floating tests up to 5.2V, and stable interfacial resistance (EIS) evolution confirmed enhanced interfacial stability and kinetics.

## Conclusions and Significance

This work successfully demonstrates a practical pathway to fabricate scalable, high-energy-density (≥600 Wh kg⁻¹), and long-cycle-life lithium metal pouch cells. The core achievement lies in the synergistic combination of: 1) an **in-situ gelated carbonate electrolyte (GMFN)** that confines solvents and suppresses parasitic reactions at both electrodes, and 2) a **scalable, multifunctional artificial SEI (BGPL)** that regulates Li plating, provides exceptional environmental stability, and simplifies Li metal processing.

The resulting 11 Ah pouch cell delivers a record specific energy of 604.2 Wh kg⁻¹ and maintains 92.83% energy retention after 100 cycles under lean electrolyte conditions, alongside significantly improved safety. This research addresses critical challenges in electrolyte depletion, interfacial instability, and Li metal processability, establishing a viable and inspiring paradigm for developing next-generation batteries for electric aviation and other high-energy-demand applications.
