## Research Background and Motivation
This research aims to address the critical challenges hindering solid-state Li-metal batteries (SSLMBs), particularly Li-S batteries, which include high interfacial resistance, poor solid-solid contact, and uncontrollable Li dendrite growth. While solid-state polymer electrolytes (SPEs) like PEO offer flexibility, they suffer from low ionic conductivity, poor mechanical strength, and unsatisfactory electrochemical stability. Existing in-situ polymerization methods are limited in monomer choice and often yield gels. This work is necessary to develop a novel SPE with intrinsic self-healing capability and an integrated electrode/electrolyte structure to ensure continuous, low-resistance interfacial contact throughout battery cycling, thereby enabling long-life, high-performance solid-state Li-S batteries.

## Research Methods and Innovation Points

### Composition of Gel Electrolyte

| Component | Specific Material | Content/Ratio |

### Polymerization Method
- **Polymerization Type**: Stepwise Condensation Polymerization (not in-situ radical polymerization within the cell)
- **Polymerization Conditions**: Temperature: 40°C, Time: ~12 h, Initiation Method: Catalyst (DBTDL) in solution.

### Key Innovation Points
1. **Self-healing Solid Polymer Electrolyte (SPE):** The poly(ether-urethane) SPE (PTMG-HDI-BHDS) incorporates dynamic covalent disulfide bonds and extensive hydrogen bonds (from urethane groups), enabling autonomous room-temperature self-healing of interfacial cracks and defects.
2. **Dual-Integrated Electrode/Electrolyte Structure:** A novel battery assembly strategy where the SPE is pre-cast onto both cathode and anode to form integrated electrodes (SPAN@SPE and Li@SPE). These are then fused together, creating a seamless, low-resistance interface that self-heals during cycling.
3. **In-operando Interface Monitoring:** Utilization of ultrasound imaging to visually monitor and confirm the superior and stable interfacial contact in the integrated structure compared to traditional laminated cells during battery operation.

### Special Performance
- **Wide-Temperature Performance**: Conductivity tested from 25 to 80°C; low activation energy suggests good performance, but extreme low-T data not provided.
- **Safety**: The integrated structure and stable interface suppress dendrites. Ultrasonic imaging confirms no gas/vacuum formation (indicating poor contact or side reactions) in integrated cells during cycling.
- **Other Characteristic Performance**: **Room-temperature autonomous self-healing** of the SPE and interfaces. Effective suppression of polysulfide shuttling in Li-S batteries, demonstrated with S@CB cathode (812 mAh g⁻¹ after 350 cycles at 0.3C).

## Mechanism Analysis and Characterization
- **XRD & Mechanical Tests**: Confirmed the amorphous nature of PTMG-HDI-BHDS (low crystallinity) and its ultra-high elongation (2000%), crucial for flexibility and interface conformity.
- **Raman Spectroscopy**: Revealed that PTMG-HDI-BHDS/LiFSI promotes a high proportion of free FSI⁻ anions (95.2%), indicating superior Li salt dissociation compared to PEO.
- **XPS Depth Profiling**: On cycled Li anodes, PTMG-HDI-BHDS formed an SEI rich in inorganic LiF (94%), which is beneficial for stable Li⁺ transfer and interface protection, unlike the organic-rich SEI from PEO.
- **Ultrasound Imaging**: Provided direct, in-operando visualization that the integrated electrode/electrolyte structure maintains excellent and even improving interfacial contact during cycling, while laminated cells show severe contact loss.
- **SEM/EDS**: Showed a seamless, integrated interface between electrode and electrolyte layers in the cross-section of pouch cells, with uniform distribution of elements (S, F).

## Conclusions and Significance
This work developed a novel self-healing poly(ether-urethane) solid polymer electrolyte (PTMG-HDI-BHDS) and a dual-integrated battery assembly strategy. The dynamic disulfide bonds and hydrogen bonds enable the SPE and the electrode/electrolyte interfaces to autonomously repair damage, maintaining excellent interfacial contact. This results in remarkably stable cycling of Li‖Li symmetric cells (>6000 h) and solid-state Li-S batteries (700 cycles with 93% capacity retention). The study introduces ultrasound imaging as a powerful tool for real-time interface monitoring. This interfacial self-healing and integration strategy effectively addresses the long-standing interfacial challenges in SSLMBs, providing a promising and practical pathway for developing high-energy-density, long-life, and safe solid-state lithium-sulfur and lithium-metal batteries.
