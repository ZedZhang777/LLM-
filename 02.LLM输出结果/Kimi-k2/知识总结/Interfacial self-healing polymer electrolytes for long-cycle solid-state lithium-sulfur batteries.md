## Research Background and Motivation

This research addresses critical challenges in solid-state lithium-sulfur batteries (SSLMBs), including limited ionic conductivity, large interfacial resistance, and unconstrained Li-dendrite growth. While solid-state polymer electrolytes (SPEs) offer better mechanical flexibility than inorganic electrolytes, they suffer from poor interfacial contact due to uneven electrode surfaces. Traditional laminated structures create high interfacial resistance and poor contact, while in-situ polymerization has limitations in monomer types and controllability. This work proposes a self-healing poly(ether-urethane)-based SPE to achieve integrated electrode/electrolyte structures with excellent interfacial contact.

## Research Methods and Innovation Points

### Composition of Gel Electrolyte

| Component | Specific Material | Content/Ratio |
|---------|---------|----------|
| **Polymer Matrix/Monomer** | Polytetrahydrofuran (PTMG, Mw=2000 g mol⁻¹) + Hexamethylene diisocyanate (HDI) + 2-hydroxyethyl disulfide (BHDS) | PTMG:HDI:BHDS = 1:2.4:1 molar ratio |
| **Lithium Salt** | Lithium bis(fluorosulfonyl)imide (LiFSI) | Polymer:LiFSI = 2:1 mass ratio |
| **Solvent** | Dichloromethane (CH₂Cl₂) + Dimethylacetamide (DMAc) | Sequential addition during synthesis |
| **Additive** | Dibutyltin dilaurate (DBTDL) | 5 μL as catalyst |
| **Initiator/Crosslinking Agent** | - | Self-polymerization via urethane formation |
| **Polymerization Conditions** | 40°C, N₂ atmosphere, total 12h reaction time |

### Polymerization Method

- **Polymerization Type**: Stepwise polycondensation (poly(ether-urethane) formation)
- **Polymerization Conditions**: 40°C in N₂-filled glove box, 1h initial reaction + 12h final reaction with DMAc additions every 2h (3 times)

### Key Innovation Points

1. **Interfacial self-healing capability**: Dynamic disulfide bonds and hydrogen bonds enable autonomous repair of solid/solid interfacial defects
2. **Dual-integrated strategy**: Pre-coating electrolyte on both electrodes creates integrated cathode@SPE and anode@SPE structures
3. **Real-time interface monitoring**: First application of ultrasound imaging to track solid-state battery interfacial contact evolution

### Special Performance

- **Wide-Temperature Performance**: Stable operation from 25-80°C with Arrhenius behavior
- **Self-healing capability**: Complete interface merging within 30 minutes at room temperature
- **Ultrasound imaging validation**: Real-time monitoring shows PPV increase from 0.58 to 0.65 V during cycling

## Mechanism Analysis and Characterization

The research employed multiple advanced characterization techniques:

- **Ultrasound imaging**: First real-time visualization of solid-state battery interfacial evolution, showing PPV changes from 0.05 V (laminated) to 0.65 V (integrated)
- **XPS depth profiling**: Revealed 94% LiF content in SEI (vs 28% in PEO systems), indicating superior interfacial stability
- **SEM/EDS**: Confirmed 3D interpenetrating Li⁺ channels between integrated electrode/electrolyte layers without delamination
- **Raman spectroscopy**: Showed 95.2% free FSI⁻ (vs 68.5% in PTMG-HDI), demonstrating superior salt dissociation
- **DFT calculations**: PTMG LUMO energy (1.35 eV) higher than PEO (0.64 eV), indicating better electrochemical stability
- **EIS analysis**: Dramatic reduction in interfacial contact resistance through dual-integrated strategy

## Conclusions and Significance

This work presents a breakthrough in solid-state battery technology through the development of self-healing poly(ether-urethane) electrolytes with dynamic disulfide bonds and hydrogen bonding networks. The dual-integrated strategy fundamentally solves the long-standing interfacial contact problems in SSLMBs by creating pre-welded electrode/electrolyte interfaces that can autonomously repair during cycling. The achieved 6000 h stable cycling in symmetric cells and 93% capacity retention over 700 cycles in Li-S batteries represent state-of-the-art performance. The innovative use of ultrasound imaging provides a powerful tool for real-time interface monitoring. This approach offers a universal strategy for constructing high-performance solid-state batteries with enhanced safety, longevity, and energy density, paving the way for practical applications in electric vehicles and grid storage systems.
