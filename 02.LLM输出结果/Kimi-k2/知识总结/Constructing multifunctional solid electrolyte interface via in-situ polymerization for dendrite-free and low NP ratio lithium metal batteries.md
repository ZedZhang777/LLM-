## Research Background and Motivation

This research addresses critical challenges in lithium metal batteries (LMBs) related to unstable solid electrolyte interface (SEI) formation. Current SEI designs suffer from non-uniform, low-flexibility film formation and limited capability to regulate Li nucleation/growth orientation, resulting in unconstrained dendrite growth and short cycling stability. The study aims to develop a multifunctional SEI that can simultaneously suppress electrolyte consumption, inhibit Li dendrite growth, and maintain stability under practical conditions including low N/P ratio (≤1.5), lean electrolyte content, and high temperature (≥60°C).

## Research Methods and Innovation Points

### Composition of Gel Electrolyte

| Component | Specific Material | Content/Ratio |
|---------|---------|----------|
| **Polymer Matrix/Monomer** | Caffeic acid (CA) polymerized layer | 1 wt% |
| **Lithium Salt** | LiTFSI | 1 M |
| **Solvent** | DOL/DME (1:1 v/v) | Base electrolyte |
| **Additive** | LiNO₃ | 2 wt% |
| **Initiator/Crosslinking Agent** | Li metal (anionic polymerization initiator) | In-situ initiation |
| **Polymerization Conditions** | Room temperature, spontaneous anionic polymerization | 24h stirring |

### Polymerization Method

- **Polymerization Type**: In-situ anionic polymerization
- **Polymerization Conditions**: Room temperature, initiated by electron transfer from Li metal to CA's C=C bonds, forming lithium caffeinate (CA-Li) polymer film

### Key Innovation Points

1. **Bioinspired Catechol Chemistry**: Employing caffeic acid (CA) containing both catechol and acrylic groups, inspired by mussel adhesion mechanisms, enabling strong Li surface adsorption and subsequent anionic polymerization.

2. **Self-Smoothing Multifunctional SEI**: Constructing hybrid organic-inorganic SEI with multiple hydrogen bonding sites for Li adsorption and steric repulsion, leading to uniform spherical Li nanosphere formation with non-preferred growth orientation.

3. **Practical Condition Adaptability**: Demonstrating stable operation under harsh conditions including high current density (10 mA cm⁻²), ultra-long cycling (8500 h), high temperature (60°C), and low N/P ratio (~1.5) with lean electrolyte (6 g Ah⁻¹).

- **Cumulative Capacity**: Achieved 4.25 Ah cm⁻² cumulative capacity over 8500 hours

## Mechanism Analysis and Characterization

The study employed comprehensive characterization techniques to elucidate the multifunctional SEI mechanism:

1. **Synchrotron 2D GIXD**: Revealed structural evolution from textured microsized Li grains to isotropic nanocrystals, eliminating preferred orientation and texture formation during cycling.

2. **XPS and TOF-SIMS**: Confirmed organic-inorganic hybrid SEI structure with CA-Li polymer layer containing RCOOLi, LiTFSI on surface, transitioning to LixNOy, LiF, Li₂O in deeper layers, with significantly reduced electrolyte decomposition.

3. **DFT Calculations**: Demonstrated strong binding energy between CA-Li and Li atoms, with steric repulsion effects constraining Li nucleation into spherical morphology, suitable for Li adsorption/desorption.

4. **SEM/AFM**: Showed uniform nanosized spherical Li deposition (vs mossy dendrites in control), smooth surface morphology, and threefold higher Young's modulus (6.61 GPa) sufficient to suppress dendrite growth.

5. **Electrochemical Analysis**: CV and EIS revealed reduced overpotential (61 vs 105 mV), suppressed electrolyte decomposition, and stable charge transfer resistance across cycling.

## Conclusions and Significance

This work presents a groundbreaking bioinspired strategy for constructing multifunctional SEI through in-situ anionic polymerization of caffeic acid additives. The resulting hybrid SEI simultaneously addresses multiple critical challenges in lithium metal batteries: dendrite suppression, electrolyte consumption reduction, and interface stability under practical conditions. The unique spherical Li nanosphere formation with non-preferred orientation represents a fundamental advancement in controlling Li deposition morphology. The demonstrated ultra-long cycling stability (8500 h), high cumulative capacity (4.25 Ah cm⁻²), and excellent performance under practical conditions (low N/P ratio, lean electrolyte, high temperature) provide a viable pathway for commercial lithium metal battery implementation. This facile and scalable additive approach offers universal applicability for high-energy-density battery systems and establishes new design principles for SEI engineering in advanced energy storage devices.
