## Research Background and Motivation

This research addresses the critical challenge of severe structural degradation in silicon monoxide (SiO) anodes caused by enormous volume expansion (>200%) during lithiation/delithiation cycles. The huge volume changes lead to particle displacement, electrode cracking, and delamination from current collectors, resulting in rapid capacity fade. While various Si-based materials and binders have been developed, the electrode-level structural collapse remains unsolved, especially under high mass loading conditions. This work proposes an innovative strategy using a supremely elastic gel polymer electrolyte (GPE) as an intra-electrode cushion to intrinsically alleviate electrode damage and maintain structural integrity.

## Research Methods and Innovation Points

### Composition of Gel Electrolyte

| Component | Specific Material | Content/Ratio |
|---------|---------|----------|
| **Polymer Matrix/Monomer** | Poly(tetramethylene ether)glycol (PTMG, Mw=2900) + 4,4'-methylene diphenyl diisocyanate (MDI) + Ethylenediamine (EDA) | PTMG:MDI:EDA = 1:2:1 molar ratio |
| **Lithium Salt** | Lithium hexafluorophosphate (LiPF₆) | 1 M in EC/DEC (1:1 v/v) |
| **Solvent** | Ethylene carbonate (EC)/Diethyl carbonate (DEC) | 1:1 v/v ratio |
| **Additive** | Fluoroethylene carbonate (FEC) | 10 wt% |
| **Initiator/Crosslinking Agent** | Self-polymerization via urethane formation | - |
| **Polymerization Conditions** | 80°C (4h) + 70°C (4h), inert argon atmosphere |

### Polymerization Method

- **Polymerization Type**: Stepwise polycondensation (poly(ether-urethane) formation)
- **Polymerization Conditions**: Two-step process: 80°C for 4h (PTMG-MDI reaction) + 70°C for 4h (EDA chain extension)

### Key Innovation Points

1. **Supreme elasticity**: Achieved >2300% elongation through unique soft-hard domain structure (PTMG soft domain + MDI-EDA hard domain)
2. **Intra-electrode cushion design**: GPE uniformly distributed within electrode to absorb volume expansion stresses
3. **Electrolyte swelling capability**: Up to 48 wt% electrolyte uptake while maintaining mechanical integrity

### Special Performance

- **Volume expansion control**: Reduced electrode thickness increase from 94% to 53% during lithiation
- **Structural integrity**: Maintained dense electrode structure without severe cracking after 20 cycles
- **High Coulombic efficiency**: Achieved 99.9% CE required for practical application (80% retention in 225 cycles)

## Mechanism Analysis and Characterization

The research employed comprehensive characterization techniques:

- **In-situ thickness measurement**: Real-time monitoring showed stable thickness evolution (20% permanent vs 52% for control)
- **SEM imaging**: Revealed minimal cracking in GPE-incorporated electrodes vs large cracks in control samples
- **XPS analysis**: Showed higher organic component (C-C/C-H, C-O) and lower inorganic salt decomposition products in SEI
- **Peel adhesion test**: Demonstrated 2× stronger adhesion (0.4 N vs 0.2 N) between active material and current collector
- **EIS monitoring**: Stable resistances over 100 cycles vs rapid resistance growth in control cells
- **AFM surface roughness**: Confirmed smooth electrode surface after GPE incorporation

## Conclusions and Significance

This work demonstrates a revolutionary approach to solving the intrinsic structural instability of Si-based anodes through the use of a supremely elastic GPE as an intra-electrode cushion. The unique copolymer design with soft-hard domain structure achieves an optimal balance between mechanical elasticity (2300% elongation) and ionic conductivity (2.4×10⁻⁴ S cm⁻¹). The GPE effectively reduces electrode expansion by 43%, maintains structural integrity without severe cracking, enables 99.9% Coulombic efficiency over 300 cycles, and achieves 70% capacity retention in full cells over 350 cycles. This strategy provides a universal platform for designing functional GPEs for practical high-performance Si-based anodes, offering significant potential for next-generation high-energy lithium-ion batteries.
