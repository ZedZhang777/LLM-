## Research Background and Motivation

This research addresses the critical challenge of uncontrolled Li dendrite growth in traditional liquid electrolytes (LEs) that impedes the commercialization of high-energy lithium metal batteries (LMBs). While gel polymer electrolytes (GPEs) and solid polymer electrolytes (SPEs) offer superior stability toward Li anodes, existing in-situ polymerization strategies typically require external initiators, special conditions like high temperature, or introduce impurities. The study aims to develop a facile strategy that can convert commercialized liquid electrolytes into quasi-solid GPEs under moderate conditions without impurity introduction, leveraging conventional materials for large-scale application.

## Research Methods and Innovation Points

### Composition of Gel Electrolyte

| Component | Specific Material | Content/Ratio |
|---------|---------|----------|
| **Polymer Matrix/Monomer** | Polydioxolane (PDXL) from DOL polymerization | 91% conversion rate |
| **Lithium Salt** | LiTFSI (1 M) + LiPF₆ (2 M) | Dual salt system |
| **Solvent** | 1,3-dioxolane (DOL)/1,2-dimethoxyethane (DME) (1:1 v/v) | Base electrolyte |
| **Additive** | Trace water (12 ppm) | Polymerization terminator |
| **Initiator/Crosslinking Agent** | LiPF₆ (self-initiating) | Cationic polymerization initiator |
| **Polymerization Conditions** | Room temperature, spontaneous | 10 hours for complete gelation |

### Polymerization Method

- **Polymerization Type**: In-situ cationic ring-opening polymerization
- **Polymerization Conditions**: Room temperature (25°C), spontaneous reaction for ~10 hours, initiated by PF₅ from LiPF₆ decomposition

### Key Innovation Points

1. **Self-Initiating System**: Commercial LiPF₆ serves as both lithium salt and initiator reservoir, releasing PF₅ that combines with trace water to form H⁺(PF₅OH)⁻ for cationic polymerization - eliminating need for external initiators.

2. **Mild Condition Operation**: Complete gelation achieved under ambient conditions without heating, high energy input, or special equipment - unprecedented simplicity for in-situ polymerization.

3. **Universal Compatibility**: Single GPE formulation demonstrates compatibility with diverse cathode systems (sulfur, LiFePO₄, NCM622) while maintaining superior electrochemical performance and safety characteristics.

### Special Performance

- **Wide-Temperature Performance**: PDXL framework with melting point of 60°C shows fine tolerance to temperature variation during battery formation
- **Safety**: Six-fold reduction in volatilization compared to LE, improved thermal stability, reduced flammability risk
- **Mechanical Robustness**: Stretchy gel characteristics enable flexible battery operation under mechanical deformation (bending/folding)

## Mechanism Analysis and Characterization

The study employed comprehensive characterization techniques to elucidate the gelation mechanism and performance improvements:

1. **NMR and FTIR Spectroscopy**: ¹H NMR confirmed PDXL structure with –CH₂–O–CH₂–CH₂–O– repeating unit (3.72 ppm and 4.75 ppm peaks), ¹³C NMR and FTIR further supported structure conformation with 91% polymerization conversion rate.

2. **GC-MS Analysis**: Detected PF₅ gas evolution from LiPF₆ decomposition, confirming the initiation mechanism for cationic polymerization.

3. **Molecular Weight Tracking**: Gel permeation chromatography showed Mn reaching ~52,000 after 10 hours, correlating with conductivity stabilization at 3.8×10⁻³ S cm⁻¹.

4. **SEM Morphology Analysis**: Revealed smooth Li surface without dendrites in GPE system vs severe pulverization and 50 μm erosion depth in LE system after cycling.

5. **XPS Characterization**: Identified LiF formation from LiPF₆ decomposition and sulfide deposits on Li surface in LE system, confirming reduced side reactions in GPE.

6. **Permeation Tests**: Demonstrated complete blockage of polysulfide diffusion in GPE vs free diffusion in LE over 48 hours, explaining suppressed shuttle effect.

7. **Electrochemical Analysis**: CV curves showed stable redox peaks in GPE vs severe peak shifting in LE, EIS confirmed stable internal resistance in GPE vs continuous increase in LE.

## Conclusions and Significance

This work presents a groundbreaking strategy for upgrading traditional liquid electrolytes into quasi-solid gel polymer electrolytes through an unprecedentedly simple in-situ cationic polymerization approach. The self-initiating system utilizing commercial LiPF₆ as both salt and initiator, operating under ambient conditions without external energy input or impurity introduction, represents a paradigm shift in electrolyte fabrication simplicity. The resulting GPE demonstrates remarkable multifunctionality: superior dendrite suppression (800+ h stable cycling), polysulfide blocking (73.7% capacity retention over 500 cycles), high-voltage compatibility (stable to 4.6V enabling NCM622 pairing), and mechanical robustness for flexible batteries. This universal electrolyte platform successfully addresses critical challenges across diverse battery chemistries (Li-S, Li-LiFePO₄, Li-NCM622) while maintaining manufacturing simplicity compatible with existing battery production infrastructure. The study establishes a new frontier for electrolyte innovation, offering a direct pathway for commercial LMB implementation through intelligent utilization of conventional materials with enhanced safety and performance characteristics.
