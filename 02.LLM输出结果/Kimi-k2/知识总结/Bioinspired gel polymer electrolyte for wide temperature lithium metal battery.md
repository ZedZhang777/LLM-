## Research Background and Motivation

This research addresses the critical challenge of achieving stable operation of lithium metal batteries with gel polymer electrolytes across extreme temperature ranges (-30 to 80°C). Conventional gel polymer electrolytes (GPEs) suffer from poor ion transport dynamics and unstable electrolyte-electrode interfaces at extreme temperatures, with insufficient ion transport at low temperatures (<25°C) and intense side reactions at high temperatures (>80°C). The study aims to develop a temperature-independent electrolyte by building weak solvation structures that can facilitate fast and uniform Li+ deposition while maintaining stable interfaces across the entire temperature range.

## Research Methods and Innovation Points

### Composition of Gel Electrolyte

| Component | Specific Material | Content/Ratio |
|---------|---------|----------|
| **Polymer Matrix/Monomer** | Poly(trifluoroethyl methacrylate) (PTFMA) | 20 wt% |
| **Lithium Salt** | Lithium bis(trifluoromethanesulfonyl)imide (LiTFSI) | 1 M |
| **Solvent** | Ethyl 3,3,3-trifluoropropanoate (FEP) | Major component |
| **Additive** | Fluoroethylene carbonate (FEC) | 5 wt% |
| **Initiator/Crosslinking Agent** | AIBN initiator, PEGDA crosslinker | 0.5 wt%, 1 wt% |
| **Polymerization Conditions** | 70°C for 5h | In-situ thermal polymerization |

### Polymerization Method

- **Polymerization Type**: In-situ radical polymerization
- **Polymerization Conditions**: 70°C for 5 hours under argon atmosphere

### Key Innovation Points

1. **Bioinspired Double Dipole Coupling**: Mimicking water grass interactions, PTFMA polymer framework forms unique dynamic non-bonding interactions with FEP coupling agent through double dipole coupling, creating brush-like polymer structure.

2. **Weak Solvation Structure Design**: Double dipole coupling regulates Li+ coordination environment by expelling FEP from first Li+ solvation sheath, forming anion-rich solvation structure similar to local high-concentration electrolytes.

3. **Wide Temperature Operation**: Enables stable Li metal battery operation from -30 to 80°C with high ionic conductivity (1.03×10⁻⁴ S cm⁻¹ at -40°C) and ultra-high Li+ transference number (0.83).

## Mechanism Analysis and Characterization

The study employed multiple advanced characterization techniques to elucidate the stabilization mechanisms:

1. **DFT Calculations and MD Simulations**: Revealed double dipole coupling between PTFMA and FEP with strongest binding energy, showing FEP enrichment around polymer brushes and anion-dominated solvation structure (CIPs 44.1%, AGGs 15.2%).

2. **Raman Spectroscopy**: Confirmed weak solvation structure with high contact ion pairs (44.1%) and ion aggregates (15.2%), minimizing solvent polarization and reducing desolvation energy.

3. **XPS Analysis**: Demonstrated formation of LiF-rich (685.1 eV) and Li₃N-rich (398.1 eV) inner SEI with organic outer layer, while CEI showed uniform LiF and Li₂O distribution with thickness of 3 nm.

4. **TOF-SIMS Depth Profiling**: Revealed homogeneous distribution of LiF throughout SEI with negligible Li₂CO₃ from solvent decomposition, confirming stable interface formation.

5. **EIS Analysis**: Showed significantly lower charge transfer resistance (Rct) and SEI resistance (RSEI) across temperature range, with exchange current density of 0.38 mA cm⁻² at -20°C.

6. **SEM and HRTEM**: Confirmed dense, smooth Li morphology without dendrites and uniform thin CEI layer on NMC811, contrasting with uneven CEI in liquid electrolyte.

## Conclusions and Significance

This work presents a groundbreaking bioinspired approach to developing wide-temperature gel polymer electrolytes through double dipole coupling strategy. The weakly solvated gel polymer electrolyte (WSGPE) successfully addresses the fundamental trade-off between ionic conductivity and interface stability across extreme temperatures. The unique solvation structure enables fast charge transfer kinetics and forms stable, LiF-rich electrode-electrolyte interfaces that effectively suppress dendrite formation and side reactions. The resulting lithium metal batteries demonstrate exceptional performance across -30 to 80°C temperature range while achieving high specific energy (490.8 Wh kg⁻¹) and enhanced safety through non-flammability. This bioinspired design concept provides a practical pathway for developing next-generation lithium metal batteries capable of operating reliably under extreme environmental conditions, with significant implications for electric vehicles, aerospace applications, and grid storage systems in diverse climates.
